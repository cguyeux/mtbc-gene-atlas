Curation note: 2026-08-31 (mtbc/Rv2566 project, litterature_review --wide back-propagation, destination C1). Valas & Bourne 2008 (J Mol Evol 66(5):494-504) identify this gene's Pfam/COG1305 domain family as 'a transglutaminase-like protein' and show a genome-wide statistical association between COG1305 domain count and the presence of an alternative bacterial proteasome (Anbu or 20S; the MTBC has the 20S): p=2.27e-08 / p=3.42e-07, replicated in proteobacteria (p=0.0045). This is a FAMILY-LEVEL claim (applies to the shared Transglut_core/COG1305 fold) and is added here for that reason. Iyer et al. 2008's more specific architectural prediction (fusion to a C-terminal carboxylate-amine/ammonia ligase domain, GS-fold/ATP-grasp) is NOT carried over to this gene: cross-checked against this atlas's own Pfam calls (mtbc/Rv2566 project P9.3), only Rv2566 among the 5 H37Rv COG1305 paralogues carries the C-terminal DUF2126 extension Iyer's example describes -- this gene is Transglut_core-only. The proteostasis-coupling hypothesis (Anbu/proteasome) has only been network-tested for Rv2566 itself (mtbc/Rv2566 P5.2, negative at the local MTBC STRING level); it has NOT been tested for this paralogue and should not be assumed to transfer without a separate check. `verdict`/`confidence` left unchanged (family_assigned/medium).

Two orthogonal outgroup signals. M. canettii (the immediate outgroup) gives a deep-divergence dN/dS (a low value confirms a constrained, real gene; shown as confident only at ≥8 substitutions, else flagged low-power). Genus-wide presence/absence (tblastn vs assembled non-MTBC genomes) places the gene on the ancient-core ↔ MTBC-specific axis: a gene absent even from the closest MTBAP relatives is a candidate MTBC-specific innovation (possible host-adaptation factor, to confirm by synteny). The phylostratum extends that axis outside the genus (tblastn vs 13 reference genomes spanning Mycobacteriaceae → Corynebacteriales → Actinomycetia → outside the phylum): it is the deepest clade in which a homolog is still detected, i.e. a proxy for gene age. Read it with the null model in mind: a shallow (young) stratum can also reflect homology-detection failure for short or fast-evolving ORFs, so it is a descriptive axis, not a proof of novelty.

Detection by mass spectrometry is direct, experimental evidence that the protein product exists — orthogonal to sequence conservation and to Tn-seq essentiality, and especially decisive for a "hypothetical" locus. Read the dataset count as abundance, not as replication. Measured across this atlas (3,491 detected proteins), the number of datasets a protein appears in tracks its abundance almost monotonically: median 1.2 ppm at 1 dataset of 16, 27 ppm at 10, 392 ppm at 15, 1,325 ppm at 16 — three orders of magnitude. A high count therefore says the protein is abundant more than it says several independent experiments confirmed it. What the count does establish, at any value, is that the product exists.

Two targeted experiments on this protein locate it outside the cytoplasm: subcellular fractionation of H37Rv probed with an antibody raised against the protein reports it predominantly in the cell-wall fraction, and immunoelectron microscopy on ultrathin sections shows it at the cell surface. Read that against the prediction on this page rather than instead of it: DeepTMHMM finds no transmembrane helix and no Sec/Tat signal peptide, and those two statements are compatible with an extracellular protein in this organism — the canonical ESX substrates EsxA/ESAT-6 and EsxB/CFP-10 are called GLOB by this very layer and are secreted. The export route of this protein is not established; the measurement says where the protein is, not how it got there.

Limits of this measurement. The fractionation blot reports only Ag85B, itself a wall-associated and secreted marker; no cytoplasmic marker (RpoB, GroEL2) is shown, and that is the control which decides whether a wall fraction is clean (this atlas measured, in P5.1b, that two published fractionated proteomes fail it). The cell-wall/cytoplasm partition therefore rests on a blot without a contamination control; the surface localisation rests on the immunoelectron microscopy, which does not depend on fraction purity. The band intensities of the fractionation figure were not re-read here: the compartment wording follows the authors' own statement ('predominantly localized on the cell wall'), not a re-quantification. The same paper's title calls the protein a serine protease on the strength of an inhibitor profile alone, with no numbered catalytic residue; that claim is NOT carried over by this field, which reports localisation only.

Regulatory edges from the ISB signed transcriptional regulatory network (TF ChIP-seq binding, Minch 2015 + TF-overexpression response, Rustad 2014). An edge is regulatory evidence (binding and/or expression change), not necessarily direct. For a "hypothetical", membership in a known regulon (e.g. DosR dormancy, PhoP virulence) is a physiological-context lead, not a function.

Condition: Condition-blind by construction. These edges come from transcription-factor OVEREXPRESSION in a single laboratory condition, so a listed regulator is not a claim that it governs this gene in every circumstance. Measured counter-examples exist within this atlas (see Rv2516c, where the DosR regulon collapses under moxifloxacin while the gene itself rises). Read a regulator as 'capable of shifting this gene', and look for the condition before building a hypothesis on it.
