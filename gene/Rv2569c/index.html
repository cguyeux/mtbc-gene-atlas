Caveat on this estimate. Bosch et al. flag this gene's vulnerability estimate as NOT certain, and here that call has a mechanical cause visible in the library itself: every guide targeting this gene is >=21 nt (str_span=0.282 on a 0-1 scale, near the genome floor), and guide repression strength saturates from 21 nt on -- so the library never sampled a partial knockdown for this gene, only the near-maximal repression plateau. The dose-response fit (and this VI) is therefore essentially unconstrained by data, not merely uncertain -- a limitation of the CRISPRi library design for the non-essential class (Bosch et al. 2021), not a defect in this specific measurement. n_guides=12 is descriptive only and does not predict this. Do not cite this VI as a standalone vulnerability claim.

Two targeted experiments on this protein locate it outside the cytoplasm: subcellular fractionation of H37Rv probed with an antibody raised against the protein reports it predominantly in the cell-wall fraction, and immunoelectron microscopy on ultrathin sections shows it at the cell surface. Read that against the prediction on this page rather than instead of it: DeepTMHMM finds no transmembrane helix and no Sec/Tat signal peptide, and those two statements are compatible with an extracellular protein in this organism — the canonical ESX substrates EsxA/ESAT-6 and EsxB/CFP-10 are called GLOB by this very layer and are secreted. The export route of this protein is not established; the measurement says where the protein is, not how it got there.

Limits of this measurement. The fractionation blot reports only Ag85B, itself a wall-associated and secreted marker; no cytoplasmic marker (RpoB, GroEL2) is shown, and that is the control which decides whether a wall fraction is clean (this atlas measured, in P5.1b, that two published fractionated proteomes fail it). The cell-wall/cytoplasm partition therefore rests on a blot without a contamination control; the surface localisation rests on the immunoelectron microscopy, which does not depend on fraction purity. The band intensities of the fractionation figure were not re-read here: the compartment wording follows the authors' own statement ('predominantly localized on the cell wall'), not a re-quantification. The same paper's title calls the protein a serine protease on the strength of an inhibitor profile alone, with no numbered catalytic residue; that claim is NOT carried over by this field, which reports localisation only.

Defined inMycobacterium marinum, gene symbol melE for this locus
Members melA · melB · melC · melD · melE (this gene)

El-Etr, Subbian, Cirillo & Cirillo 2004 (Infect Immun 72(12):6902-6913, PMID 15557611, PMC529147) identified the mel1 cosmid region in M. marinum by a gain-of-function screen (cosmid library transformed into M. smegmatis, screened for enhanced macrophage association) and then pinpointed melE by transposon mutagenesis (Tn22) and allelic exchange: the M. marinum melE mutant is defective for cell association with the murine (J774A.1) and fish (CLC) macrophage cell lines, and this defect is complemented by expressing the M. tuberculosis Rv2569c gene itself from the hsp60 promoter (construct pJDC77). melA, melB, melC and melD were annotated ONLY by sequence homology to their M. tuberculosis counterparts (Table 1 of the paper) and were NEVER individually mutated or tested in this study -- the direct functional evidence in mel1 concerns melE/Rv2569c alone.

'mel1' names a conserved gene SET (near-identical order across M. marinum and the MTBC, per the source paper), not a demonstrated operon: El-Etr et al. 2004 measured neither a shared transcript nor co-expression. A single five-gene transcription unit is not even geometrically possible in H37Rv: melC (Rv2567) sits on the '+' strand while melD (Rv2568c) and melE (Rv2569c, this gene) sit on the '-' strand. Only melD-melE are adjacent and co-directional (8 bp gap), which the co-directional heuristic above already captures as a 2-gene predicted operon -- and Roback et al. 2007 (published_operon_evidence, above) assigns that specific pair a LOW probability (0.011) and flags disagreement with the heuristic. melA (Rv2560) is not even adjacent to melB (Rv2566) in H37Rv: five unrelated ORFs intervene (Rv2561-Rv2565, including a glutamine ABC transporter).

Transcriptional regulation (signed TRN: ChIP-seq + TFOE)

Regulated by (1 TF) Rv1990c (represses†)

† marks an edge (1 of 1 listed) whose response to overexpression of that TF stays below the authors' criterion for a significant effect (two-fold change, p ≤ 0.01). The network keeps any change of at least 20 %, so “activates” or “represses” gives the direction of a modest change that is not significant on its own (hover for the values and the ChIP-seq label).

Regulatory edges from the ISB signed transcriptional regulatory network, as compiled for Peterson et al. 2019: TF ChIP-seq binding in the promoter window of the gene or of a member of its operon (Minch 2015), kept only where the gene changes by at least 20 % when the TF is overexpressed (Rustad 2014). An edge is thus physical and functional evidence together, but not necessarily direct. For a "hypothetical", membership in a known regulon (e.g. DosR dormancy, PhoP virulence) is a physiological-context lead, not a function.

Condition: Condition-blind by construction. Each sign is the direction of an expression change on transcription-factor OVEREXPRESSION in a single laboratory condition (Rustad 2014), so a listed regulator is not a claim that it governs this gene in every circumstance. Measured counter-examples exist within this atlas (see Rv2516c, where the DosR regulon collapses under moxifloxacin while the gene itself rises). The network keeps any change of at least 20 %: an edge marked with a dagger stays below the authors' criterion for a significant overexpression effect (two-fold change and p <= 0.01), or is the TF's own transcript, which the overexpression construct raises by itself. For such an edge 'activates' or 'represses' is the direction of a modest change that is not significant on its own. Read a regulator as 'capable of shifting this gene' mainly for edges without a dagger, and look for the condition before building a hypothesis on it.

Where the experimental evidence comes from, and why it matters here. STRING's experimental channel fuses interactions measured in M. tuberculosis with interactions measured in another organism and transferred to this pair by orthology. The two are not equivalent for this atlas, whose standing rule is that better studied elsewhere is not a function established in M. tuberculosis. Measured on the served network: of the 38 229 partner pairs shown across the atlas, 13 483 partner rows carry a non-zero experimental channel, and only 106 pairs rest on an interaction measured in M. tuberculosis itself — 0.3 % of the network. Those 106 are long-established complexes (ESX-1/ESAT-6, RNA polymerase with CarD and RbpA, the Pup–Mpa proteasome, gyrase, Emb, GarA with PknB and PknG, the Rel modules); no still-uncharacterised gene is among them. The reason is not a gap in this atlas but in the literature: there is almost no experimental interactome of this bacterium, IntAct holding 134 curated protein pairs for a ~4 000-gene proteome. A exp (transferred) badge is therefore a lead, never a measurement, and an absence of badge is not evidence that the pair does not interact.
