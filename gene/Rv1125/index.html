Revised (this work)WS/DGAT domain-containing protein. Pfam: WS_DGAT_C (PF06974.19, residues 270-413, 34.4 bits vs GA 20.7). The CATALYTIC domain of the family (PF03007, WS_DGAT_cat) is not annotated by UniProt but is not absent either: an unthresholded hmmsearch scores it at 15.7 bits over residues 8-168, below the 23.6-bit gathering threshold, against 276.4 bits for tgs1/Rv3130c and no hit at all for the neighbouring ephC/Rv1124 (mtbc/Rv1125, piste P8, 2026-09-06). The HHxxxDG motif (137-143) lies in this sub-threshold catalytic region, not in the annotated C-terminal domain. Unrestricted Foldseek search (AFDB-swissprot) confirms WS/DGAT as the best absolute structural match (top hits: MTBC Tgs/DGAT paralogs TM=0.71-0.79, and A. baylyi WSD Q8GGG1 TM=0.73), not merely the only candidate tested. ESM-1v masked-marginal LLR (Meier et al. 2021) on the aligned catalytic dyad shows a residue-level gradient: His138/Asp142 (matching the conserved WS/DGAT catalytic pair H133/D137, the more critical residue per Stoveken et al. 2009) fall in the 90-100th percentile of evolutionary constraint against a 20-point background scan, while Ala137 (the position of the lost first histidine) sits near the background median (60-65th percentile). CORRECTION 2026-09-06 (mtbc/Rv1125, piste P10): the glycine of the HHxxxDG motif is Gly143, not Ser147 -- the motif is contiguous (residues 137-143 read A-H-A-L-G-D-G), and the 147 pairing came from the 14%-identity structural alignment to 7NXG, which inserts a 4-residue gap between the D and the G that none of the 12 MTBC paralogs shows. Gly143 is conserved and constrained (ESM-1v LLR -8.4 for G143S, 83rd percentile), so Rv1125 retains three of the four motif positions and has lost only the first histidine. CAVEAT (solvent-accessibility follow-up, Bio.PDB.SASA on the local AlphaFold model): all 4 catalytic/motif positions are buried (RSA 0.009-0.233, Gly143 marginally), as are the two most-constrained background positions (D10, Y405) -- burial alone explains much of the ESM-1v signal, so the gradient is consistent with EITHER a modified-but-real catalytic site OR generic structural/folding importance; ESM-1v and RSA together do not distinguish the two. Not proof of activity -- only a direct enzymatic assay could settle it. Source: mtbc/Rv1125 project, piste P1.3.f/P1.5.a/P1.5.b. UPDATE (mtbc/Rv1125 project, pistes P1.6-P1.8, 2026-09-01-02): comparison to the 12 other experimentally characterized MTBC Tgs/DGAT paralogs shows the loss of the first catalytic histidine (Ala137 replacing His132 of the canonical HHxxxDG motif) is not unique to Rv1125 -- one paralog, Rv2484c (itself the least active gene of the Daniel et al. 2004 screen), independently shares it. Cross-species polarization by targeted tblastn against 8 NTM/MTBAP/Corynebacteriales orthologs (up to 69% identity, far above the 14% of the only prior structural template) shows 7 of 8 orthologs retain the same alanine, including two of three genomes entirely outside genus Mycobacterium (Gordonia bronchialis, Tsukamurella paurometabola; the sole exception, Mycobacteroides abscessus, carries a serine, a further independent substitution rather than a reversion) -- while His138, Asp142 and Gly143 are conserved without exception in all 8. This places the loss of the first catalytic histidine at or before the divergence of Corynebacteriales, at least 200 million years ago: not a recent, Rv1125-specific degeneration, but the ancestral state of an entire structural lineage. Six independent computational methods across two registers (structural/evolutionary modelling: ESM-1v, RSA/SASA, Boltz-2 docking, paralog comparison, McDonald-Kreitman with M. canettii outgroup; and cross-species orthology) all leave catalytic specificity open rather than resolving it -- only direct enzymatic or mutagenesis data could settle whether this is a genuine but mechanistically distinct acyltransferase or an enzyme losing its catalytic function. SEPARATELY, this gene appeared as a candidate in a doctoral-thesis GWAS of tuberculosis clinical form (pulmonary vs meningeal; La PhD thesis 2023), whose candidates concentrate in lipid-biosynthesis pathways. Its only common variation in the complex is 3 linked SNPs (NC_000962.3:1248936/1248382/1248978), and re-genotyping all 142,694 genomes of the group's curated database shows these are EXACT CLADE MARKERS with no within-lineage variation anywhere: ~100% in lineages 1, 2, 3, 5-9 and the animal lineages, 1/54,539 and 0/54,539 in lineage 4 for the first two, and 82.65% for the third in lineage 4, resolving entirely at the sub-lineage partition (0% in L4.8 and L4.9, ~100% elsewhere). Their ancestral state is corroborated in M. canettii (95-96% of 153 genomes). A mono-locus association is therefore not identifiable at this locus: the variation IS clade membership. pN/pS from segregating SNPs (singletons removed) normalised by possible sites. Low pN/pS = purifying selection (a strong signal that a "hypothetical" is a real, constrained gene). A high pN/pS is ambiguous: relaxed constraint or positive selection (drug resistance, antigenic variation) inflate it; e.g. rpoB/katG/pncA score high here for resistance, not loss of function. A clonal disruption (one allele over a clade) suggests lineage pseudogenisation; a convergent one (many independent alleles) is typical of resistance loss-of-function.

Two orthogonal outgroup signals. M. canettii (the immediate outgroup) gives a deep-divergence dN/dS (a low value confirms a constrained, real gene; shown as confident only at ≥8 substitutions, else flagged low-power). Genus-wide presence/absence (tblastn vs assembled non-MTBC genomes) places the gene on the ancient-core ↔ MTBC-specific axis: a gene absent even from the closest MTBAP relatives is a candidate MTBC-specific innovation (possible host-adaptation factor, to confirm by synteny). The phylostratum extends that axis outside the genus (tblastn vs 13 reference genomes spanning Mycobacteriaceae → Corynebacteriales → Actinomycetia → outside the phylum): it is the deepest clade in which a homolog is still detected, i.e. a proxy for gene age. Read it with the null model in mind: a shallow (young) stratum can also reflect homology-detection failure for short or fast-evolving ORFs, so it is a descriptive axis, not a proof of novelty.

Detection by mass spectrometry is direct, experimental evidence that the protein product exists — orthogonal to sequence conservation and to Tn-seq essentiality, and especially decisive for a "hypothetical" locus. Read the dataset count as abundance, not as replication. Measured across this atlas (3,491 detected proteins), the number of datasets a protein appears in tracks its abundance almost monotonically: median 1.2 ppm at 1 dataset of 16, 27 ppm at 10, 392 ppm at 15, 1,325 ppm at 16 — three orders of magnitude. A high count therefore says the protein is abundant more than it says several independent experiments confirmed it. What the count does establish, at any value, is that the product exists.
