Revised (this work)Essential (Tn-seq ES, DeJesus et al. 2017; robust to a dedicated read-mappability test) and intrinsically disordered (44.8% disordered residues by metapredict v3 + AlphaFold pLDDT, two IDRs) protein of 183 aa. Physically positioned between sigH (Rv3223c) and rshA (Rv3221A) -- it overlaps both by 4 bp on each side, and has no promoter of its own, being transcribed from the sigH promoter -- but this physical position is NOT equivalent to membership of the SigH regulon or of a functionally demonstrated SigH-RshA operon: Rv3222c is absent from both published SigH regulon definitions (Sharp et al. 2016; Mehra & Kaushal 2009), and the sigH-rshA-Rv3222c operon structure itself is only PREDICTED, never functionally demonstrated to include this gene. Rv3222c is the last coding survivor of a small paralogous gene family degraded by a single ancestral insertion of an IS110-family mobile element (Pfam PF01548): three additional copies survive in H37Rv only as non-coding decayed relics (one disrupted mid-CDS by the insertion itself), and the gene shows stronger purifying selection than these relics. No detectable homology for the protein itself (HHpred no confident hit, Pfam hmmscan --cut_ga 0 hit, Foldseek 0 hit against the full AlphaFold/PDB databases, absent from the ESM Atlas). The previously reported CRISPRi vulnerability index (-15.5) is UNRELIABLE for this gene and should not be cited as a vulnerability claim: Bosch et al. 2021 flag it certain=False/crispr_ess=NonEssential, and its 14 guides are amongst the weakest in the genome (best guide strength 0.430 vs a genome median of 0.960 for the per-gene maximum) -- the underlying model never observed this gene under strong depletion, so the score is an out-of-domain extrapolation, not a measurement. Not previously functionally characterised in the literature: full-text search finds only two pre-2025 mentions of the locus tag, both traceable to a single 2007 locus-tag error (Thakur et al. 2007, JBC) that mislabelled Rv3222c as the anti-sigma factor of SigH, an error the real anti-sigma factor RshA/Rv3221A already carries; UniProt and the core SigH-regulon literature are unaffected. Full account, evidence and caveats: Guyeux C., submitted 2026-08-13 (Journal of Bacteriology), 'Rv3222c, the unnamed gene between sigH and rshA, encodes an essential intrinsically disordered protein and is the last intact member of a degenerate gene family in Mycobacterium tuberculosis'.

CRISPRi vulnerability

Vulnerability index -15.54 (95% CI -17.97 to -12.96). A more negative index = more vulnerable to CRISPRi knockdown. The source defines no cut-off for a “highly vulnerable” gene: the index is a continuous measure, to be read against its reference population. Among the genes whose estimate the source flags as confident, more than half have an index of -6 or below, so -6 does not set a gene apart. The index measures vulnerability to knockdown, not druggability.

Quantitative CRISPRi knockdown, graded (finer than binary Tn-seq essentiality). Source: CRISPRi vulnerability index (Bosch 2021, supplementary Table S2 'mmc3.xlsx', bulk import via Europe PMC -- supersedes the per-gene pebble.rockefeller.edu scrape of phase46_crispri_vulnerability.py).

Caveat on this estimate. Bosch et al. flag this gene's vulnerability estimate as NOT certain (their own confidence call, not a p-value threshold). Its guide library did sample a range of repression strengths (str_span=0.430 on a 0-1 scale, thanks to at least one guide <=18 nt; n_guides=14 descriptive only), so the dose-response fit is not starved of titration the way most 'not certain' genes are -- the uncertainty here is the authors' own confidence call on the fit, not a design limitation of the guide library. Do not cite this VI as a standalone vulnerability claim without checking `certain` first.

Two orthogonal outgroup signals. M. canettii (the immediate outgroup) gives a deep-divergence dN/dS (a low value confirms a constrained, real gene; shown as confident only at ≥8 substitutions, else flagged low-power). Genus-wide presence/absence (tblastn vs assembled non-MTBC genomes) places the gene on the ancient-core ↔ MTBC-specific axis: a gene absent even from the closest MTBAP relatives is a candidate MTBC-specific innovation (possible host-adaptation factor, to confirm by synteny). The phylostratum extends that axis outside the genus (tblastn vs 13 reference genomes spanning Mycobacteriaceae → Corynebacteriales → Actinomycetia → outside the phylum): it is the deepest clade in which a homolog is still detected, i.e. a proxy for gene age. Read it with the null model in mind: a shallow (young) stratum can also reflect homology-detection failure for short or fast-evolving ORFs, so it is a descriptive axis, not a proof of novelty.

Essentiality (transposon mutagenesis) essential

The two screens disagree on this gene: Tn-seq says essential (ES), CRISPRi says non-essential, with an estimate its own authors flag as not certain.

Two genome-wide screens disagree on this gene, and both are kept as they stand. They do not measure the same thing: the Tn-seq call (DeJesus 2017) reports tolerance to transposon insertion across the ORF, the CRISPRi call (Bosch 2021) reports tolerance to partial knockdown. A gene can absorb an insertion and still be required at full dosage, or the reverse. Here the CRISPRi estimate is one its own authors flag as NOT certain (vulnerability index -15.542), so it is the weaker of the two calls. Do not treat the CRISPRi essentiality as settled here. Proteomics (mass spectrometry) not detected

Not detected in the mass-spectrometry datasets integrated by PaxDb. This is not evidence of absence, and this atlas can say which bias dominates, because it measured it on its own 482 undetected proteins. Size is the main factor: undetected proteins have a median length of 145 aa against 295 aa for detected ones, and 25.3 % of them are shorter than 100 aa against 6.0 %. Predicted localisation matters far less than usually assumed: detection rates are 89.3 % for globular, 84.9 % for transmembrane, 88.0 % for lipoproteins and 77.8 % for signal-peptide proteins — so a short protein is under-sampled whatever its predicted compartment, and hydrophobicity alone is a weak excuse. Condition-specific expression and few tryptic peptides (PE/PPE repeats) add to this.

Conditions covered. By the names PaxDb gives them, the aggregated datasets include log phase and starvation phase cultures (Albrethsen 2013), late exponential cultures, whole-cell lysates, control cultures of H37Rv and of a SAM-Mtb strain, a pool of MTBC lineages, the Schubert 2013 complete-proteome library and PeptideAtlas, GPM and Cho/Cortes compilations. “Never seen” therefore means never seen in these culture conditions, not in other conditions such as infection.

Observable peptides. An in-silico trypsin digest of this protein (7–30 aa, no missed cleavage) gives 5 observable peptides; the median is 13 for detected proteins and 6 for undetected ones in this atlas. The 16 is the number of datasets aggregated by PaxDb, not the number of datasets that searched for this protein: an undetected protein has no row to count.

Length. This protein is 183 aa long. In this atlas, 12.6 % of the proteins in its length class (150–199 aa: 49 of 389) are not detected by the MS datasets integrated by PaxDb, against 12.2 % overall (483 of 3974) and 6.3 % for proteins of 300 aa or longer (116 of 1837). Non-detection is therefore about as frequent at this length as overall; this is a base rate for the length class, not a measure for this gene.

The network keeps any change of at least 20 %, so “activates” or “represses” gives the direction of a modest change that is not significant on its own (hover for the values and the ChIP-seq label).

Regulatory edges from the ISB signed transcriptional regulatory network, as compiled for Peterson et al. 2019: TF ChIP-seq binding in the promoter window of the gene or of a member of its operon (Minch 2015), kept only where the gene changes by at least 20 % when the TF is overexpressed (Rustad 2014). An edge is thus physical and functional evidence together, but not necessarily direct. For a "hypothetical", membership in a known regulon (e.g. DosR dormancy, PhoP virulence) is a physiological-context lead, not a function.

Condition: Condition-blind by construction. Each sign is the direction of an expression change on transcription-factor OVEREXPRESSION in a single laboratory condition (Rustad 2014), so a listed regulator is not a claim that it governs this gene in every circumstance. Measured counter-examples exist within this atlas (see Rv2516c, where the DosR regulon collapses under moxifloxacin while the gene itself rises). The network keeps any change of at least 20 %: an edge marked with a dagger stays below the authors' criterion for a significant overexpression effect (two-fold change and p <= 0.01), or is the TF's own transcript, which the overexpression construct raises by itself. For such an edge 'activates' or 'represses' is the direction of a modest change that is not significant on its own. Read a regulator as 'capable of shifting this gene' mainly for edges without a dagger, and look for the condition before building a hypothesis on it.

Where the experimental evidence comes from, and why it matters here. STRING's experimental channel fuses interactions measured in M. tuberculosis with interactions measured in another organism and transferred to this pair by orthology. The two are not equivalent for this atlas, whose standing rule is that better studied elsewhere is not a function established in M. tuberculosis. Measured on the served network: of the 38 229 partner pairs shown across the atlas, 13 483 partner rows carry a non-zero experimental channel, and only 106 pairs rest on an interaction measured in M. tuberculosis itself — 0.3 % of the network. Those 106 are long-established complexes (ESX-1/ESAT-6, RNA polymerase with CarD and RbpA, the Pup–Mpa proteasome, gyrase, Emb, GarA with PknB and PknG, the Rel modules); no still-uncharacterised gene is among them. The reason is not a gap in this atlas but in the literature: there is almost no experimental interactome of this bacterium, IntAct holding 134 curated protein pairs for a ~4 000-gene proteome. A exp (transferred) badge is therefore a lead, never a measurement, and an absence of badge is not evidence that the pair does not interact.
