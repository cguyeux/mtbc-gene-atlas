Curation note: P52.13 (2026-11-03), reserve on the annotated start; the coordinates, the protein sequence and the verdict of this page are unchanged and still follow the annotated start (residue numbers on this page, Pfam positions included, count from it). Nothing we could read supports the annotated start, and its first 50 residues (rich in Pro, Ser, Ala and Arg, no domain; the Pfam hydrolase domains begin at residues 80 to 82) are probably not part of the protein. Three observations. (1) Annotated orthologues: among 43 annotated non-tuberculous mycobacterial genomes (RefSeq), 42 give a reciprocal-best-hit orthologue, and in all 42 the annotated CDS begins 50 to 81 residues after the start annotated here (median 66.5; 19 of 42 at 50 or 52 residues). Measured the same way, none of 53 randomly drawn genes has a median offset above 25 residues. (2) RNA: a leaderless transcript starts at residue 51 (position 2506428, GTG; the `internal_tss` of the transcription start section, Cortes et al. 2013, listed again as a leaderless ORF with the same stop codon by Smith et al. 2022, Supplementary File 1A, class Isoform). Both entries come from the same TSS map and count as one signal. (3) Translation: Ribo-RET (Smith et al. 2022, Supplementary File 1C) calls no start at the annotated start. That window is not masked by the protocol (no leaderless TSS of Supplementary File 1A lies within 80 nt of it), but Ribo-RET calls recover only 35 % (1025 of 2931) of the proteins known to be produced (detected by mass spectrometry in this atlas) that are not on the leaderless list, so this silence is weak evidence against the annotated start. At residue 51 no call was possible: the Ribo-RET analysis of Smith et al. sets the coverage to zero over the 50 nt that follow each of the 1285 leaderless transcription start sites of Supplementary File 1A (here residues 51 to 67), so the absence of a call at residue 51 is a consequence of the protocol and says nothing about whether that start is used. The initiation site is therefore somewhere between residue 51 and residue 82, and the data read here do not choose between them; the DeJesus et al. 2013 candidate listed in the transcription start section (residue 40, position 2506395) matches neither. With the annotated start this gene overlaps the antisense Rv2231B (vapB16) over 106 nt (overlap section); a start at residue 37 or later removes that overlap, which bears on the overlap only and not on whether Rv2231B encodes a protein. Limits: an offset between orthologues does not by itself prove a wrong start (an N-terminal extension can be gained or lost in one lineage); the 43 genomes are unevenly spread over the genus (they include groups of close relatives, for example M. marinum, M. ulcerans, M. liflandii and M. pseudoshottsii) and RefSeq annotations partly rely on related proteins, so the 42 orthologues are not 42 independent measurements; the thresholds were set after looking at four species; no N-terminal peptide position was read, and one tryptic peptide inside residues 1 to 50 would refute this reserve. Transcriptional regulation (signed TRN: ChIP-seq + TFOE)

Regulated by (1 TF) Rv1353c (represses)

Regulatory edges from the ISB signed transcriptional regulatory network, as compiled for Peterson et al. 2019: TF ChIP-seq binding in the promoter window of the gene or of a member of its operon (Minch 2015), kept only where the gene changes by at least 20 % when the TF is overexpressed (Rustad 2014). An edge is thus physical and functional evidence together, but not necessarily direct. For a "hypothetical", membership in a known regulon (e.g. DosR dormancy, PhoP virulence) is a physiological-context lead, not a function.

Condition: Condition-blind by construction. Each sign is the direction of an expression change on transcription-factor OVEREXPRESSION in a single laboratory condition (Rustad 2014), so a listed regulator is not a claim that it governs this gene in every circumstance. Measured counter-examples exist within this atlas (see Rv2516c, where the DosR regulon collapses under moxifloxacin while the gene itself rises). The network keeps any change of at least 20 %: an edge marked with a dagger stays below the authors' criterion for a significant overexpression effect (two-fold change and p <= 0.01), or is the TF's own transcript, which the overexpression construct raises by itself. For such an edge 'activates' or 'represses' is the direction of a modest change that is not significant on its own. Read a regulator as 'capable of shifting this gene' mainly for edges without a dagger, and look for the condition before building a hypothesis on it.

Where the experimental evidence comes from, and why it matters here. STRING's experimental channel fuses interactions measured in M. tuberculosis with interactions measured in another organism and transferred to this pair by orthology. The two are not equivalent for this atlas, whose standing rule is that better studied elsewhere is not a function established in M. tuberculosis. Measured on the served network: of the 38 229 partner pairs shown across the atlas, 13 483 partner rows carry a non-zero experimental channel, and only 106 pairs rest on an interaction measured in M. tuberculosis itself — 0.3 % of the network. Those 106 are long-established complexes (ESX-1/ESAT-6, RNA polymerase with CarD and RbpA, the Pup–Mpa proteasome, gyrase, Emb, GarA with PknB and PknG, the Rel modules); no still-uncharacterised gene is among them. The reason is not a gap in this atlas but in the literature: there is almost no experimental interactome of this bacterium, IntAct holding 134 curated protein pairs for a ~4 000-gene proteome. A exp (transferred) badge is therefore a lead, never a measurement, and an absence of badge is not evidence that the pair does not interact.
