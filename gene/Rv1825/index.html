CRISPRi vulnerability

Vulnerability index 1.44 (95% CI -0.74 to 4.48). A more negative index = more vulnerable to CRISPRi knockdown. The source defines no cut-off for a “highly vulnerable” gene: the index is a continuous measure, to be read against its reference population. Among the genes whose estimate the source flags as confident, more than half have an index of -6 or below, so -6 does not set a gene apart. The index measures vulnerability to knockdown, not druggability.

Quantitative CRISPRi knockdown, graded (finer than binary Tn-seq essentiality). Source: CRISPRi vulnerability index (Bosch 2021, supplementary Table S2 'mmc3.xlsx', bulk import via Europe PMC -- supersedes the per-gene pebble.rockefeller.edu scrape of phase46_crispri_vulnerability.py).

Caveat on this estimate. Bosch et al. flag this gene's vulnerability estimate as NOT certain, and here that call has a mechanical cause visible in the library itself: every guide targeting this gene is >=21 nt (str_span=0.222 on a 0-1 scale, near the genome floor), and guide repression strength saturates from 21 nt on -- so the library never sampled a partial knockdown for this gene, only the near-maximal repression plateau. The dose-response fit (and this VI) is therefore essentially unconstrained by data, not merely uncertain -- a limitation of the CRISPRi library design for the non-essential class (Bosch et al. 2021), not a defect in this specific measurement. n_guides=8 is descriptive only and does not predict this. Do not cite this VI as a standalone vulnerability claim.

† marks an edge (1 of 1 listed) whose response to overexpression of that TF stays below the authors' criterion for a significant effect (two-fold change, p ≤ 0.01). The network keeps any change of at least 20 %, so “activates” or “represses” gives the direction of a modest change that is not significant on its own (hover for the values and the ChIP-seq label).

Regulatory edges from the ISB signed transcriptional regulatory network, as compiled for Peterson et al. 2019: TF ChIP-seq binding in the promoter window of the gene or of a member of its operon (Minch 2015), kept only where the gene changes by at least 20 % when the TF is overexpressed (Rustad 2014). An edge is thus physical and functional evidence together, but not necessarily direct. For a "hypothetical", membership in a known regulon (e.g. DosR dormancy, PhoP virulence) is a physiological-context lead, not a function.

Condition: Condition-blind by construction. Each sign is the direction of an expression change on transcription-factor OVEREXPRESSION in a single laboratory condition (Rustad 2014), so a listed regulator is not a claim that it governs this gene in every circumstance. Measured counter-examples exist within this atlas (see Rv2516c, where the DosR regulon collapses under moxifloxacin while the gene itself rises). The network keeps any change of at least 20 %: an edge marked with a dagger stays below the authors' criterion for a significant overexpression effect (two-fold change and p <= 0.01), or is the TF's own transcript, which the overexpression construct raises by itself. For such an edge 'activates' or 'represses' is the direction of a modest change that is not significant on its own. Read a regulator as 'capable of shifting this gene' mainly for edges without a dagger, and look for the condition before building a hypothesis on it.

STRING combines evidence channels (neighborhood, fusion, cooccurrence, coexpression, experimental, database, text-mining) into a 0–1000 score. The ctx badge marks edges carried by the genomic-context channels (conserved neighborhood, fusion, phylogenetic co-occurrence), which are independent of orthology and structure and the strongest signal for an unknown gene. The no text-mining column recomputes the score from data alone, so a link that does not depend on the literature is visible. Association is a function hypothesis, not proof: corroborate with the operon context and the primary literature before assigning a function.

Where the experimental evidence comes from, and why it matters here. STRING's experimental channel fuses interactions measured in M. tuberculosis with interactions measured in another organism and transferred to this pair by orthology. The two are not equivalent for this atlas, whose standing rule is that better studied elsewhere is not a function established in M. tuberculosis. Measured on the served network: of the 38 229 partner pairs shown across the atlas, 13 483 partner rows carry a non-zero experimental channel, and only 106 pairs rest on an interaction measured in M. tuberculosis itself — 0.3 % of the network. Those 106 are long-established complexes (ESX-1/ESAT-6, RNA polymerase with CarD and RbpA, the Pup–Mpa proteasome, gyrase, Emb, GarA with PknB and PknG, the Rel modules); no still-uncharacterised gene is among them. The reason is not a gap in this atlas but in the literature: there is almost no experimental interactome of this bacterium, IntAct holding 134 curated protein pairs for a ~4 000-gene proteome. A exp (transferred) badge is therefore a lead, never a measurement, and an absence of badge is not evidence that the pair does not interact.

  • HHpred web (MPI Bioinformatics Toolkit, profile-profile remote homology), interpreted in project 'Still unknown gene function', 2026-06-10. A fold/family-level assignment, not a demonstrated function.
  • SigF regulon (Williams 2007, Hartkoorn 2012 ChIP-on-chip+microarray): induction demonstrated only at OPERON level (Rv1823-Rv1826, promoter upstream of Rv1823); no probe/primer in either study resolves Rv1825 individually -- gene-level regulation not established.
  • Independent computational corroboration of the cell-envelope/divisome context (Doerks 2012, comparative genomic-context annotation, method independent of this atlas's own HHpred/STRING pipeline): Rv1823/Rv1825 annotated 'hypothetical cell wall associated gene products' functionally linked to pgsA2 in a conserved actinobacterial operon.
  • Candidate UDP-binding site (Bhagavat 2017, PocketMatch PMSmax 0.46) refuted on two independent fronts: sub-threshold in the source study's own candidate table (its stated significance cutoff is PMSmax >=0.5), and no structural convergence to the UDP-glycosyltransferase template (PDB 3T7M) on Foldseek re-analysis (e-value ~2-3, TM-score ~0.07-0.08, site-residue conservation p=0.63 vs null) -- consolidates, without contradicting, the divisome/FtsB-FtsL fold assignment.
  • Clade-restricted missense variant p.Arg53Trp (NC_000962.3:2071191:C:T, 0-based) fixed at 100% across 1415 strains spanning 7 subgroups of the MTBC basal African clade (animal-adapted lineages + L6/L9/L10), 0% among 123,789 strains of the strict human lineages (L1-L4, L7, L8, M. canettii). Independently corroborated by a second, unrelated phylogenomic reconstruction (Chiner-Oms et al. 2022, Dataset S8, >9000 genomes, distinct tree-building pipeline): the same substitution is placed on the ancestral branch of lineages A1+lineage6, with no recurrent homoplasy detected in either tree.
  • A second missense variant at the same locus (p.Pro181Ser, NC_000962.3:2071575:C:T) flagged as destabilising in CHOPIN (Ochoa-Montano 2015) is unrelated to the above: an independent L4 sub-lineage marker (~12-13% of L4, 0% in the basal African clade), confirmed on two independent strain panels (local curated database and an ~256,000-genome external corpus) and by the same Chiner-Oms 2022 dataset.
  • Operon synteny (pgsA2-Rv1823-Rv1824-Rv1825) conserved beyond the genus Mycobacterium: intact and ordered in all 51/51 local MTBC+NTM+outgroup genomes, then in Rhodococcus (Corynebacteriales, 2/2, ~52% id) and, at greater phylogenetic distance, in Streptomyces coelicolor, Bifidobacterium longum and Cutibacterium acnes (33-35% id, 4/4 ordered); orthology confirmed by reciprocal BLAST against the full H37Rv proteome. Method transposed from Goodall et al. 2023 (PLOS Genetics), independently applied here for the first time to this operon (tblastn e-5 on 71 local genomes, project Rv1825 P2.13, cahier 2026-09-18).
  • Predicted, not measured, physical association: local Boltz-2 co-folding of Rv1825 with each of its two immediate operon neighbours, one job and one MSA-server run per pair. Rv1825xRv1824: ipTM 0.71-0.80 and minimum inter-chain PAE 2.9-5.0 A over 15 diffusion samples (0.74-0.78 and 3.1-4.2 A in the first 3-sample run); Rv1825xRv1823: ipTM 0.61-0.63 and minimum inter-chain PAE 0.9-1.3 A over 15 samples (0.62-0.64 and 1.1-1.2 A over 3). PAE is the minimum over the inter-chain block (best residue pair), the project's convention, not a mean. 'Reproducible' means that these confidence scores are stable across diffusion samples; the geometry is not: the distance from residue 53 to Rv1824 falls in two groups (10/15 samples at 3.4-9.6 A, 5/15 at 17-23 A), i.e. at least two poses. The reference positive control (Rv1025-divIC/FtsB-ftsQ, ipTM 0.38, minimum PAE 5.1 A, KB tuberculosis.md 2026-07-05) was run in AlphaFold3, not Boltz-2: it is a reference point, not a calibration of these scores. The specificity controls are 3-sample Boltz-2 runs (a 3-sample run reversed the R53W result once 15 samples were drawn, project Rv1825 P2.16): gcvH (genomic neighbour, not a coiled-coil) is unstable, ipTM 0.27-0.67, PAE 6.2-16.4 A; Rv1682 (SMC-like ATPase, soluble, unrelated to the divisome) gives ipTM 0.34-0.40, which equals the reference positive control, with PAE 8.3-12.7 A. Rv1823 is a DUF881 paralogue of Rv1825, so generic packing between related coiled-coils is not excluded for that pair. No experimental support: STRING has no experimental or curated-database evidence for either pair (neighbourhood, co-occurrence, co-expression and text-mining channels only); the Humphreys 2024 predicted interactome lists Rv1825-Rv1824 (AF_score 0.92) and not Rv1825-Rv1823 (a filtered set, so the absence is not informative). Project Rv1825 P2.14 and P2.16, cahier 2026-09-18/19 and 2026-09-20; caveat on coiled-coil specificity controls versioned to KB tuberculosis.md (2026-09-19).
  • Published serodiagnostic antigen, independently of any divisome function: Zhou et al. 2015 (dedicated protein-array screen) identify Rv1825 among 8 'newly identified' antigens; Zhou et al. 2021 (same group, explicit follow-up) test it individually among 9 antigens with published numbers (sensitivity 15.6%, 95%CI 9.8-23.1; specificity 95.3%, 95%CI 86.9-99.0).
