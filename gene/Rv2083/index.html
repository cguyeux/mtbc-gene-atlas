Why this gene is still served as functionally unknown. The evidence behind the “functional” label here is: interaction network (proximity labelling).

The label rests on a proximity-labelling proteomics study (split ALFA-tag nanobody fused to TurboID) that finds this protein among those biotinylated near the tagged PacL1 of the metal-resistance effluxosome (PMID 41688792; log2 fold change 1.22, -log10 p 1.53). Proximity is not a direct physical interaction. It is a real datum and a genuine lead, but guilt by association is one evidence handle, and the project's two-handle policy asks for a second before a verdict moves.

1 TB publication mentions this gene. A proximity-labelling study of the metal-resistance "effluxosome" (split ALFA-tag nanobody fused to TurboID, two PacL1-ALFA baits against a MalF(1-2)-ALFA membrane control) lists Rv2083 among seven proteins biotinylated at least twofold more with both baits (PacL1, Rv1265, CtpC, PPE20, MoeX, Rv2083, AhpC); Table 1 of the paper gives Rv2083 a log2 fold change of 1.22 and a -log10 p value of 1.53 (about p = 0.03). Proximity labelling reports closeness to the tagged PacL1 in the membrane, not a direct physical interaction, and the authors read the enrichment of PacL1, CtpC, CtpV and PacL3 as confirming that these belong to the effluxosome, whereas the other enriched proteins, Rv2083 among them, are described as a broader network that the effluxosome could extend to. The source paper's own table labels Rv2083 as a "PPE family protein" -- **not corroborated** by this atlas's Pfam scan (no domain hit), nor by Mycobrowser ("conserved hypothetical") or UniProt ("uncharacterised"); PE/PPE proteins are known to escape standard Pfam gathering thresholds through sequence repetitiveness, so the absence of a Pfam hit is weaker evidence here than for a typical dark gene, but the family assignment still needs a dedicated check (HHpred or relaxed-threshold Pfam against the PE/PPE profiles specifically) before any fiche update. The proximity-labelling enrichment itself is independent of that question and is the stronger part of this lead. reformulation, not a quotation

Vulnerability index 0.73 (95% CI -0.23 to 2.24). A more negative index = more vulnerable to CRISPRi knockdown. The source defines no cut-off for a “highly vulnerable” gene: the index is a continuous measure, to be read against its reference population. Quantitative CRISPRi knockdown, graded (finer than binary Tn-seq essentiality). Source: CRISPRi vulnerability index (Bosch 2021, supplementary Table S2 'mmc3.xlsx', bulk import via Europe PMC -- supersedes the per-gene pebble.rockefeller.edu scrape of phase46_crispri_vulnerability.py).

Caveat on this estimate. Bosch et al. flag this gene's vulnerability estimate as NOT certain, and here that call has a mechanical cause visible in the library itself: every guide targeting this gene is >=21 nt (str_span=0.338 on a 0-1 scale, near the genome floor), and guide repression strength saturates from 21 nt on -- so the library never sampled a partial knockdown for this gene, only the near-maximal repression plateau. The dose-response fit (and this VI) is therefore essentially unconstrained by data, not merely uncertain -- a limitation of the CRISPRi library design for the non-essential class (Bosch et al. 2021), not a defect in this specific measurement. n_guides=6 is descriptive only and does not predict this. Do not cite this VI as a standalone vulnerability claim.

Read the disruption percentage against its ceiling, not against 100%. The strain collection is deeply imbalanced by clade, so a loss fixed in an entire lineage still shows a small overall fraction: a gene disrupted in every L6 strain caps at 0.96% of the collection, in every M. caprae strain at 0.20%, in every M. microti strain at 0.02%. Of the 705 clades in this collection, 689 have a ceiling below 1%. A figure of "0.15% of strains" is therefore uninterpretable on its own — it can be noise or a complete, lineage-defining loss. Use the clade attribution row above, which gives the sub-clade sizes, and never read the percentage alone.

pN/pS from segregating SNPs (singletons removed) normalised by possible sites. Low pN/pS = purifying selection (a strong signal that a "hypothetical" is a real, constrained gene). A high pN/pS is ambiguous: relaxed constraint or positive selection (drug resistance, antigenic variation) inflate it; e.g. rpoB/katG/pncA score high here for resistance, not loss of function. A clonal disruption (one allele over a clade) suggests lineage pseudogenisation; a convergent one (many independent alleles) is typical of resistance loss-of-function.

Two orthogonal outgroup signals. M. canettii (the immediate outgroup) gives a deep-divergence dN/dS (a low value confirms a constrained, real gene; shown as confident only at ≥8 substitutions, else flagged low-power). Genus-wide presence/absence (tblastn vs assembled non-MTBC genomes) places the gene on the ancient-core ↔ MTBC-specific axis: a gene absent even from the closest MTBAP relatives is a candidate MTBC-specific innovation (possible host-adaptation factor, to confirm by synteny). The phylostratum extends that axis outside the genus (tblastn vs 13 reference genomes spanning Mycobacteriaceae → Corynebacteriales → Actinomycetia → outside the phylum): it is the deepest clade in which a homolog is still detected, i.e. a proxy for gene age. Read it with the null model in mind: a shallow (young) stratum can also reflect homology-detection failure for short or fast-evolving ORFs, so it is a descriptive axis, not a proof of novelty.

Where the experimental evidence comes from, and why it matters here. STRING's experimental channel fuses interactions measured in M. tuberculosis with interactions measured in another organism and transferred to this pair by orthology. The two are not equivalent for this atlas, whose standing rule is that better studied elsewhere is not a function established in M. tuberculosis. Measured on the served network: of the 38 229 partner pairs shown across the atlas, 13 483 partner rows carry a non-zero experimental channel, and only 106 pairs rest on an interaction measured in M. tuberculosis itself — 0.3 % of the network. Those 106 are long-established complexes (ESX-1/ESAT-6, RNA polymerase with CarD and RbpA, the Pup–Mpa proteasome, gyrase, Emb, GarA with PknB and PknG, the Rel modules); no still-uncharacterised gene is among them. The reason is not a gap in this atlas but in the literature: there is almost no experimental interactome of this bacterium, IntAct holding 134 curated protein pairs for a ~4 000-gene proteome. A exp (transferred) badge is therefore a lead, never a measurement, and an absence of badge is not evidence that the pair does not interact.
