Proteomics (mass spectrometry) not detected

Not detected in the mass-spectrometry datasets integrated by PaxDb. This is not evidence of absence, and this atlas can say which bias dominates, because it measured it on its own 482 undetected proteins. Size is the main factor: undetected proteins have a median length of 145 aa against 295 aa for detected ones, and 25.3 % of them are shorter than 100 aa against 6.0 %. Predicted localisation matters far less than usually assumed: detection rates are 89.3 % for globular, 84.9 % for transmembrane, 88.0 % for lipoproteins and 77.8 % for signal-peptide proteins — so a short protein is under-sampled whatever its predicted compartment, and hydrophobicity alone is a weak excuse. Condition-specific expression and few tryptic peptides (PE/PPE repeats) add to this.

Conditions covered. By the names PaxDb gives them, the aggregated datasets include log phase and starvation phase cultures (Albrethsen 2013), late exponential cultures, whole-cell lysates, control cultures of H37Rv and of a SAM-Mtb strain, a pool of MTBC lineages, the Schubert 2013 complete-proteome library and PeptideAtlas, GPM and Cho/Cortes compilations. “Never seen” therefore means never seen in these culture conditions, not in other conditions such as infection.

Observable peptides. An in-silico trypsin digest of this protein (7–30 aa, no missed cleavage) gives 5 observable peptides; the median is 13 for detected proteins and 6 for undetected ones in this atlas. The 16 is the number of datasets aggregated by PaxDb, not the number of datasets that searched for this protein: an undetected protein has no row to count.

Length. This protein is 113 aa long. In this atlas, 24.6 % of the proteins in its length class (100–149 aa: 126 of 512) are not detected by the MS datasets integrated by PaxDb, against 12.2 % overall (483 of 3974) and 6.3 % for proteins of 300 aa or longer (116 of 1837). Non-detection is therefore much more frequent at this length, whatever the protein does; this is a base rate for the length class, not a measure for this gene.

A verified absence of signals, which is not a compartment. No Sec/Tat signal peptide, no transmembrane helix and no lipobox were found on this sequence. The predictor reports topology and export signals; it does not measure where the protein ends up, so this page draws no cytoplasmic conclusion from it. In M. tuberculosis that reservation is not theoretical: the 12 Esx paralogues of this atlas — EsxA/ESAT-6 and EsxB/CFP-10 among them, the best-established secreted proteins of the species — carry this very call, because substrates of the type VII (ESX) secretion system leave no recognisable signal. « Secreted » and « no signal peptide » are true together here.

† marks an edge (1 of 1 listed) whose response to overexpression of that TF stays below the authors' criterion for a significant effect (two-fold change, p ≤ 0.01). The network keeps any change of at least 20 %, so “activates” or “represses” gives the direction of a modest change that is not significant on its own (hover for the values and the ChIP-seq label).

Regulatory edges from the ISB signed transcriptional regulatory network, as compiled for Peterson et al. 2019: TF ChIP-seq binding in the promoter window of the gene or of a member of its operon (Minch 2015), kept only where the gene changes by at least 20 % when the TF is overexpressed (Rustad 2014). An edge is thus physical and functional evidence together, but not necessarily direct. For a "hypothetical", membership in a known regulon (e.g. DosR dormancy, PhoP virulence) is a physiological-context lead, not a function.

Condition: Condition-blind by construction. Each sign is the direction of an expression change on transcription-factor OVEREXPRESSION in a single laboratory condition (Rustad 2014), so a listed regulator is not a claim that it governs this gene in every circumstance. Measured counter-examples exist within this atlas (see Rv2516c, where the DosR regulon collapses under moxifloxacin while the gene itself rises). The network keeps any change of at least 20 %: an edge marked with a dagger stays below the authors' criterion for a significant overexpression effect (two-fold change and p <= 0.01), or is the TF's own transcript, which the overexpression construct raises by itself. For such an edge 'activates' or 'represses' is the direction of a modest change that is not significant on its own. Read a regulator as 'capable of shifting this gene' mainly for edges without a dagger, and look for the condition before building a hypothesis on it.

STRING combines evidence channels (neighborhood, fusion, cooccurrence, coexpression, experimental, database, text-mining) into a 0–1000 score. The ctx badge marks edges carried by the genomic-context channels (conserved neighborhood, fusion, phylogenetic co-occurrence), which are independent of orthology and structure and the strongest signal for an unknown gene. The no text-mining column recomputes the score from data alone, so a link that does not depend on the literature is visible. Association is a function hypothesis, not proof: corroborate with the operon context and the primary literature before assigning a function.

Where the experimental evidence comes from, and why it matters here. STRING's experimental channel fuses interactions measured in M. tuberculosis with interactions measured in another organism and transferred to this pair by orthology. The two are not equivalent for this atlas, whose standing rule is that better studied elsewhere is not a function established in M. tuberculosis. Measured on the served network: of the 38 229 partner pairs shown across the atlas, 13 483 partner rows carry a non-zero experimental channel, and only 106 pairs rest on an interaction measured in M. tuberculosis itself — 0.3 % of the network. Those 106 are long-established complexes (ESX-1/ESAT-6, RNA polymerase with CarD and RbpA, the Pup–Mpa proteasome, gyrase, Emb, GarA with PknB and PknG, the Rel modules); no still-uncharacterised gene is among them. The reason is not a gap in this atlas but in the literature: there is almost no experimental interactome of this bacterium, IntAct holding 134 curated protein pairs for a ~4 000-gene proteome. A exp (transferred) badge is therefore a lead, never a measurement, and an absence of badge is not evidence that the pair does not interact.
