CRISPRi vulnerability

Vulnerability index 1.47 (95% CI -0.01 to 3.96). A more negative index = more vulnerable to CRISPRi knockdown. The source defines no cut-off for a “highly vulnerable” gene: the index is a continuous measure, to be read against its reference population. Among the genes whose estimate the source flags as confident, more than half have an index of -6 or below, so -6 does not set a gene apart. The index measures vulnerability to knockdown, not druggability.

Quantitative CRISPRi knockdown, graded (finer than binary Tn-seq essentiality). Source: CRISPRi vulnerability index (Bosch 2021, supplementary Table S2 'mmc3.xlsx', bulk import via Europe PMC -- supersedes the per-gene pebble.rockefeller.edu scrape of phase46_crispri_vulnerability.py).

Caveat on this estimate. Bosch et al. flag this gene's vulnerability estimate as NOT certain, and here that call has a mechanical cause visible in the library itself: every guide targeting this gene is >=21 nt (str_span=0.291 on a 0-1 scale, near the genome floor), and guide repression strength saturates from 21 nt on -- so the library never sampled a partial knockdown for this gene, only the near-maximal repression plateau. The dose-response fit (and this VI) is therefore essentially unconstrained by data, not merely uncertain -- a limitation of the CRISPRi library design for the non-essential class (Bosch et al. 2021), not a defect in this specific measurement. n_guides=7 is descriptive only and does not predict this. Do not cite this VI as a standalone vulnerability claim.

Read the disruption percentage against its ceiling, not against 100%. The strain collection is deeply imbalanced by clade, so a loss fixed in an entire lineage still shows a small overall fraction: a gene disrupted in every L6 strain caps at 0.96% of the collection, in every M. caprae strain at 0.20%, in every M. microti strain at 0.02%. Of the 705 clades in this collection, 689 have a ceiling below 1%. A figure of "0.15% of strains" is therefore uninterpretable on its own — it can be noise or a complete, lineage-defining loss. Use the clade attribution row above, which gives the sub-clade sizes, and never read the percentage alone.

pN/pS from segregating SNPs (singletons removed) normalised by possible sites. Low pN/pS = purifying selection (a strong signal that a "hypothetical" is a real, constrained gene). A high pN/pS is ambiguous: relaxed constraint or positive selection (drug resistance, antigenic variation) inflate it; e.g. rpoB/katG/pncA score high here for resistance, not loss of function. A clonal disruption (one allele over a clade) suggests lineage pseudogenisation; a convergent one (many independent alleles) is typical of resistance loss-of-function.

Two orthogonal outgroup signals. M. canettii (the immediate outgroup) gives a deep-divergence dN/dS (a low value confirms a constrained, real gene; shown as confident only at ≥8 substitutions, else flagged low-power). Genus-wide presence/absence (tblastn vs assembled non-MTBC genomes) places the gene on the ancient-core ↔ MTBC-specific axis: a gene absent even from the closest MTBAP relatives is a candidate MTBC-specific innovation (possible host-adaptation factor, to confirm by synteny). The phylostratum extends that axis outside the genus (tblastn vs 13 reference genomes spanning Mycobacteriaceae → Corynebacteriales → Actinomycetia → outside the phylum): it is the deepest clade in which a homolog is still detected, i.e. a proxy for gene age. Read it with the null model in mind: a shallow (young) stratum can also reflect homology-detection failure for short or fast-evolving ORFs, so it is a descriptive axis, not a proof of novelty.

Proteomics (mass spectrometry) not detected

Not detected in the mass-spectrometry datasets integrated by PaxDb. This is not evidence of absence, and this atlas can say which bias dominates, because it measured it on its own 482 undetected proteins. Size is the main factor: undetected proteins have a median length of 145 aa against 295 aa for detected ones, and 25.3 % of them are shorter than 100 aa against 6.0 %. Predicted localisation matters far less than usually assumed: detection rates are 89.3 % for globular, 84.9 % for transmembrane, 88.0 % for lipoproteins and 77.8 % for signal-peptide proteins — so a short protein is under-sampled whatever its predicted compartment, and hydrophobicity alone is a weak excuse. Condition-specific expression and few tryptic peptides (PE/PPE repeats) add to this.

Conditions covered. By the names PaxDb gives them, the aggregated datasets include log phase and starvation phase cultures (Albrethsen 2013), late exponential cultures, whole-cell lysates, control cultures of H37Rv and of a SAM-Mtb strain, a pool of MTBC lineages, the Schubert 2013 complete-proteome library and PeptideAtlas, GPM and Cho/Cortes compilations. “Never seen” therefore means never seen in these culture conditions, not in other conditions such as infection.

Observable peptides. An in-silico trypsin digest of this protein (7–30 aa, no missed cleavage) gives 13 observable peptides; the median is 13 for detected proteins and 6 for undetected ones in this atlas. The 16 is the number of datasets aggregated by PaxDb, not the number of datasets that searched for this protein: an undetected protein has no row to count.

Length. This protein is 263 aa long. In this atlas, 7.6 % of the proteins in its length class (200–299 aa: 69 of 904) are not detected by the MS datasets integrated by PaxDb, against 12.2 % overall (483 of 3974) and 6.3 % for proteins of 300 aa or longer (116 of 1837). Non-detection is therefore less frequent at this length than overall, so length alone is a weaker explanation here; this is a base rate for the length class, not a measure for this gene.

Transcriptional regulation (signed TRN: ChIP-seq + TFOE)

Regulated by (3 TF) Rv0324 (activates) · ramB (represses†) · Rv2989 (activates)

† marks an edge (1 of 3 listed) whose response to overexpression of that TF stays below the authors' criterion for a significant effect (two-fold change, p ≤ 0.01). The network keeps any change of at least 20 %, so “activates” or “represses” gives the direction of a modest change that is not significant on its own (hover for the values and the ChIP-seq label).

Regulatory edges from the ISB signed transcriptional regulatory network, as compiled for Peterson et al. 2019: TF ChIP-seq binding in the promoter window of the gene or of a member of its operon (Minch 2015), kept only where the gene changes by at least 20 % when the TF is overexpressed (Rustad 2014). An edge is thus physical and functional evidence together, but not necessarily direct. For a "hypothetical", membership in a known regulon (e.g. DosR dormancy, PhoP virulence) is a physiological-context lead, not a function.

Condition: Condition-blind by construction. Each sign is the direction of an expression change on transcription-factor OVEREXPRESSION in a single laboratory condition (Rustad 2014), so a listed regulator is not a claim that it governs this gene in every circumstance. Measured counter-examples exist within this atlas (see Rv2516c, where the DosR regulon collapses under moxifloxacin while the gene itself rises). The network keeps any change of at least 20 %: an edge marked with a dagger stays below the authors' criterion for a significant overexpression effect (two-fold change and p <= 0.01), or is the TF's own transcript, which the overexpression construct raises by itself. For such an edge 'activates' or 'represses' is the direction of a modest change that is not significant on its own. Read a regulator as 'capable of shifting this gene' mainly for edges without a dagger, and look for the condition before building a hypothesis on it.

The two are not equivalent for this atlas, whose standing rule is that better studied elsewhere is not a function established in M. tuberculosis. Measured on the served network: of the 38 229 partner pairs shown across the atlas, 13 483 partner rows carry a non-zero experimental channel, and only 106 pairs rest on an interaction measured in M. tuberculosis itself — 0.3 % of the network. Those 106 are long-established complexes (ESX-1/ESAT-6, RNA polymerase with CarD and RbpA, the Pup–Mpa proteasome, gyrase, Emb, GarA with PknB and PknG, the Rel modules); no still-uncharacterised gene is among them. The reason is not a gap in this atlas but in the literature: there is almost no experimental interactome of this bacterium, IntAct holding 134 curated protein pairs for a ~4 000-gene proteome. A exp (transferred) badge is therefore a lead, never a measurement, and an absence of badge is not evidence that the pair does not interact.
