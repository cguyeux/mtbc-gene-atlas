Annotation: from legacy to revised

Legacy (H37Rv / Mycobrowser)membrane protein
MTBC0 PGAP re-annotationDUF3566 domain-containing protein
Revised (this work)Actinobacterial CofA-family membrane protein (Pfam DUF3566, 2 TM helices). Reported to interact with the transmembrane domain of PonA2 (Rv3682) -- the M. tuberculosis ortholog of Corynebacterium glutamicum PBP1a -- and explicitly NOT with that of PonA1 (Rv0050), the PBP1b ortholog, in a heterologous POLAR two-hybrid assay in E. coli using full-length Mtb CofA as prey; the authors themselves describe this signal as weak, and it is strengthened when the Mtb-specific N-terminal extension (residues 1-190) is deleted (Sher, Lim & Bernhardt 2020, eLife, 10.7554/eLife.54761). CofA is proposed to act as a conserved partner promoting aPBP-mediated peptidoglycan synthesis, paralleling its role with PBP1a in C. glutamicum. Note that one sentence of that paper's Results attributes the CofA-DeltaN gain to PonA1; its own Discussion, its figure-supplement title and its orthology statements all say PonA2, so the partner is reported here as PonA2 only. Genetic support is indirect: Tn-seq shows Mtb CofA becomes less dispensable when the NON-cognate aPBP PonA1 is inactivated, which the primary source reads -- through the known PonA1-PonA2 synthetic lethality -- as consistent with CofA being required for PonA2 function (Kieser et al. 2015, PNAS, PMID 26438867). No physical interaction involving CofA, PonA1 or PonA2 is recorded in curated interaction databases (see evidence). Also consistent with this atlas's own phenotype layer (Tn-seq): altered fitness specifically under meropenem (a PBP/peptidoglycan-synthesis-targeting antibiotic, lfc -5.02) and in mouse infection (day 45, lfc -7.24) -- both noted in this atlas before this literature connection was made. "CofA" is the name used by the primary source but is not yet an official NCBI/UniProt gene symbol for the M. tuberculosis locus; treated here as a functional annotation, not a confirmed nomenclature assignment.

In the literature (TB corpus sweep) characterised phenotype

2 TB publications mention this gene. Named **CofA** by Sher, Lim & Bernhardt (eLife 2020): a conserved actinobacterial cofactor for the bifunctional class-A PBP cell wall synthases (aPBPs). In a heterologous POLAR two-hybrid assay in E. coli, full-length Mtb CofA gives a weak interaction signal with the transmembrane domain of **PonA2** (Rv3682, the Mtb ortholog of Cglu PBP1a) and explicitly none with that of PonA1 (Rv0050, the PBP1b ortholog); deleting the Mtb-specific N-terminal extension strengthens it. Genetic support is indirect and concerns the OTHER paralog: Tn-seq shows Mtb CofA becomes less dispensable when the non-cognate aPBP PonA1 is inactivated, which the authors read -- through the known PonA1-PonA2 synthetic lethality -- as consistent with CofA being required for PonA2 function. Protein-fusion/localisation experiments place CofA at the division septum alongside its aPBP partner, conserved across the Corynebacterineae. A secondary 2025 report (Sci Rep, Tn-seq screen for rifampicin/streptomycin tolerance) independently lists Rv0007 as peptidoglycan synthesis and describes a genetic interaction with **ponA2** specifically -- consistent with the primary source, now verified against its full text. Consistent with the existing transmembrane domain (DUF3566) and TnSeq phenotypes (required in vivo, in vitro passage, altered under meropenem). A phenotype/genetic-interaction is not itself a solved molecular mechanism: treat CofA as a strong, named lead for a dedicated deep-dive, not a settled function. reformulation, not a quotation

Two orthogonal outgroup signals. M. canettii (the immediate outgroup) gives a deep-divergence dN/dS (a low value confirms a constrained, real gene; shown as confident only at ≥8 substitutions, else flagged low-power). Genus-wide presence/absence (tblastn vs assembled non-MTBC genomes) places the gene on the ancient-core ↔ MTBC-specific axis: a gene absent even from the closest MTBAP relatives is a candidate MTBC-specific innovation (possible host-adaptation factor, to confirm by synteny). The phylostratum extends that axis outside the genus (tblastn vs 13 reference genomes spanning Mycobacteriaceae → Corynebacteriales → Actinomycetia → outside the phylum): it is the deepest clade in which a homolog is still detected, i.e. a proxy for gene age. Read it with the null model in mind: a shallow (young) stratum can also reflect homology-detection failure for short or fast-evolving ORFs, so it is a descriptive axis, not a proof of novelty.

Detection by mass spectrometry is direct, experimental evidence that the protein product exists — orthogonal to sequence conservation and to Tn-seq essentiality, and especially decisive for a "hypothetical" locus. Read the dataset count as abundance, not as replication. Measured across this atlas (3,491 detected proteins), the number of datasets a protein appears in tracks its abundance almost monotonically: median 1.2 ppm at 1 dataset of 16, 27 ppm at 10, 392 ppm at 15, 1,325 ppm at 16 — three orders of magnitude. A high count therefore says the protein is abundant more than it says several independent experiments confirmed it. What the count does establish, at any value, is that the product exists.

Evidence

  • POLAR two-hybrid assay (heterologous, in E. coli; bait = isolated aPBP transmembrane domain): full-length MtbCofA gives a weak interaction signal with the TM domain of PonA2 (Rv3682, residues 1-41) but NOT with that of PonA1 (Rv0050, residues 120-167); deleting the Mtb-specific N-terminal extension (1-190) strengthens the signal (Sher et al. 2020, eLife, PMID 32167475, Figure 6D and Figure 6-figure supplement 1).
  • Internal inconsistency of the primary source, resolved in favour of PonA2: one Results sentence names PonA1 for the CofA-DeltaN gain, while the Discussion, the figure-supplement title ("Interaction of full length MtbCofA with PonA2") and the orthology notes in the Figure 6D legend all name PonA2 (full text re-read 2026-09-05).
  • No curated physical-interaction trace: IntAct queried directly returns 0 interactions for each of CofA (P9WMA7), PonA1 (P71707) and PonA2 (I6YGX2); in this atlas's own STRING layer, all 15 partners of Rv0007 have experimental=0, and neither PonA1 nor PonA2 appears among them (companion project mtbc/Rv0007, 2026-09-03).
  • Family assignment is independent of that debate: Pfam DUF3566 (PF12089) at i-E 1.7e-41 over residues 187-302, plus residue-level alignment of the C-terminal module against the characterised C. glutamicum ortholog CofA/cgp_0016 (UniProt Q8NUC5, 114 aa, DUF3566): 34.3% identity over 105 aligned positions (BLOSUM62 local), the aligned window covering both DeepTMHMM helices (TM1 206-226, TM2 260-282) and 93% of the C. glutamicum protein. The N-terminal 1-186 extension is disordered and specific to Mtb, absent from the ortholog (companion project mtbc/Rv0007, 2026-09-03; not published in this form elsewhere).
  • Independent Tn-seq genetic interaction: MtbCofA (Rv0007) becomes less dispensable upon inactivation of the non-cognate aPBP PonA1, read by the primary source as consistent with a requirement for PonA2 function via the known PonA1-PonA2 synthetic lethality (Kieser et al. 2015, PNAS, PMID 26438867).
  • Consistent with this atlas's own Tn-seq phenotype data (present before this connection was made): altered fitness under meropenem and in mouse infection.
  • Found via full-text-only literature sweep (P16.2a-septies, phase138, 2026-08-17) -- invisible to the title+abstract PubMed sweep because the locus tag appears only in a figure legend.
