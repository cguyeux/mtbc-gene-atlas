Mollenkopf et al. chose Rv2520c among 36 DNA vaccine candidates because the gene is present in BCG while the protein was not detected in the BCG proteome on 2D gels, and call it a transmembrane protein of unknown function of 8.6 kDa. In mice its protection was intermediate and not significantly different from the vector control in most experiments (the text concludes that it failed to induce reliable protection), and as a boost it did not significantly add to BCG. It induced IFN-gamma at day 28 but not at day 35, and high IgG1 and IgG2 titres. A vaccine-candidate result in mice, not a function. 2026-10-02 (mtbc/Rv2520c project, back-propagation P52.2, destination C1, status: published). Bertholet et al. produced Rv2520c as one of 94 recombinant H37Rv proteins screened for human T-cell antigens (Table I): His-tagged in E. coli and purified to more than 90% homogeneity. The authors report that every antigen of the panel was recognised by at least 44% of PPD-positive donors (IFN-gamma recall, 18 donors); this floor belongs to the panel, not to Rv2520c, whose own response is only in a figure. The protein can be produced recombinantly and is seen by T cells of PPD-positive donors; nothing is said of its function. 2026-10-02 (mtbc/Rv2520c project, back-propagation P52.2, destination C1, status: in preparation, not peer-reviewed). Read with care. This protein (75 aa) is below the size where this detector has meaningful power: on proven enzymes, only 3.8% (1/26) under 200 aa reach this layer's P2Rank confidence threshold of best_probability >= 0.70, versus 60.5% (75/124) above it (P16.3b calibration, negative control EsxA/EsxB-scale panel). Both figures are recall AT 0.70, which is also the best separation this detector reaches on that panel (Youden J = 0.416) and whose false-positive rate above 200 aa is 18.9% (81.1% specificity); the looser 0.50 that appears elsewhere in this repository would raise recall above 200 aa to 74.2% and drop specificity to 64.9%. A negative or weak pocket result here should NOT be read as evidence against a ligand-binding role -- the test essentially has no power at this length, not that the protein lacks a site. The same holds in the other direction, and that half was missing here: a STRONG score at this length is not a result either. The same calibration panel does carry that other arm below 200 aa, and the two do not separate: the 3.8% above is matched by 6.6% (5/76) of proteins annotated as non-catalytic clearing 0.70 (Fisher p = 1.00; 15.4% against 11.8% at 0.50, p = 0.73). At this length the score therefore carries no measured information in either direction -- which is a stronger statement than untested power, and the reason a strong score here is not a result. Caveat on this estimate. Bosch et al. flag this gene's vulnerability estimate as NOT certain, and here that call has a mechanical cause visible in the library itself: every guide targeting this gene is >=21 nt (str_span=0.403 on a 0-1 scale, near the genome floor), and guide repression strength saturates from 21 nt on -- so the library never sampled a partial knockdown for this gene, only the near-maximal repression plateau. The dose-response fit (and this VI) is therefore essentially unconstrained by data, not merely uncertain -- a limitation of the CRISPRi library design for the non-essential class (Bosch et al. 2021), not a defect in this specific measurement. n_guides=5 is descriptive only and does not predict this. Do not cite this VI as a standalone vulnerability claim.

Where the experimental evidence comes from, and why it matters here. STRING's experimental channel fuses interactions measured in M. tuberculosis with interactions measured in another organism and transferred to this pair by orthology. The two are not equivalent for this atlas, whose standing rule is that better studied elsewhere is not a function established in M. tuberculosis. Measured on the served network: of the 38 229 partner pairs shown across the atlas, 13 483 partner rows carry a non-zero experimental channel, and only 106 pairs rest on an interaction measured in M. tuberculosis itself — 0.3 % of the network. Those 106 are long-established complexes (ESX-1/ESAT-6, RNA polymerase with CarD and RbpA, the Pup–Mpa proteasome, gyrase, Emb, GarA with PknB and PknG, the Rel modules); no still-uncharacterised gene is among them. The reason is not a gap in this atlas but in the literature: there is almost no experimental interactome of this bacterium, IntAct holding 134 curated protein pairs for a ~4 000-gene proteome. A exp (transferred) badge is therefore a lead, never a measurement, and an absence of badge is not evidence that the pair does not interact.
