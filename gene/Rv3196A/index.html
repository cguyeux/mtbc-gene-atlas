Revised (this work)No molecular function is established, and the verdict stays dark. What the evidence supports is a STATUS, not a function: a small (66 aa), highly abundant protein (733 ppm, 91.5th percentile, detected in 14 of 16 PaxDb datasets) that reaches the extracytoplasmic space and is seen by the immune system. (i) Peptides derived from Rv3196A are presented on MHC class I during infection (Sher et al., eLife 2023, 10.7554/elife.84070, Discussion). (ii) The M. marinum ortholog MMAR_1367 (UniProt B2HFI1, 84.2% identity) is quantified in the SECRETED protein fraction of every one of the 13 deletion-mutant-versus-wild-type comparisons of the ESX-1 proteo-genetic survey of Cronin et al. (PNAS 2022, 10.1073/pnas.2123100119, Dataset S2 -- table row read here, not merely cited), and its secretion is NOT ESX-1-dependent (log2 fold change -0.11 to +0.80, average P >= 0.46, the authors' own threshold never met, including in the deltaeccCb1, deltaesxA and deltaesxB strains). (iii) Rv3196A itself is reported as detected by MS in M. tuberculosis culture filtrates (Bell et al. 2012, 10.1021/pr2007939); that statement is cited by Sher et al. and could NOT be verified here (closed access, no preprint, supplementary tables unreachable). The protein carries no signal peptide and no transmembrane helix (DeepTMHMM, this atlas), so if it is exported it is by a route that leaves no recognisable signal -- which is precisely the reading Sher et al. propose. No Pfam domain, no confident HHpred hit (best E = 8.4, incoherent cohort), no active-site match, singleton operon. Transcriptionally it is strongly inducible: log2 fold change +3.76 in an alkX (rv3249c) deletion mutant, the second highest of the 41 upregulated genes (adjusted P = 1.77e-30; Microbiol Spectrum 2022, 10.1128/spectrum.01969-22, Table 1), and 1.51-fold (pH 5.7) / 1.57-fold (pH 7.0) at 5% versus 0.5% CO2 (Infect Immun 2025, 10.1128/iai.00568-24, Table 1). Read together: an antigenic protein of unknown molecular function, plausibly but not demonstrably exported. Antigenicity and export are a status; neither is a molecular function, and neither requalifies this gene.

Curation note: 2026-09-08 (P45.5). First hand curation of this locus. Rv3196A is the gene that triggered the whole P45 full-text campaign and was the only one not to be instructed by it; its fiche still served the automatic sentence "Function unknown. Foldseek best (non-significant) hit: ... human Nup49 coiled-coil", a hit whose own probability (0.66) makes it noise. Three things were verified rather than repeated. Cronin et al. 2022, which eLife cites for the M. marinum ortholog, was identified (PNAS, ESX-1 proteo-genetics) and its Dataset S2 read: MMAR_1367 is in the secreted fraction of all 13 mutants and is not ESX-1-dependent -- a positive fact AND a negative one, both new to this fiche. Spectrum 2022 turned out not to be a passing table mention but a rank-2 induction (log2 FC 3.76) in an alkX mutant. Bell et al. 2012 stays cited-not-verified, and is written as such. HYPOTHESIS RAISED AND THEN REJECTED, recorded because the negative is informative. That alkX RNA-seq looks like a WhiB7 response: rank 1 is whiB7 (Rv3197A) and ranks 3, 5, 7 and 11 are erm(37), Rv1258c, eis and Rv0263c -- five members of the WhiB7 regulon -- with Rv3196A at rank 2. So: is Rv3196A a WhiB7-regulon gene? Three checks, all negative. (a) This atlas's own co-expression compendium (647 RNA-seq samples, iModulonDB) DOES contain a WhiB7 iModulon of 8 genes, and it recovers the canonical set -- whiB7, eis, erm(37), Rv1258c, Rv0263c. Rv3196A is not in it; its only iModulon is Unc_4, which contains none of those. The positive control shows the test could have said yes. (b) The chromosomal argument is weaker than proximity suggests: Rv3196A and whiB7 are ~1.5 kb apart on the same (minus) strand, but Rv3197 lies between them ON THE OPPOSITE strand (gaps 127 bp and 33 bp), so read-through from the whiB7 locus is not a simple explanation. (c) The atlas regulation layer lists only Rv0767c and CANNOT see WhiB7 (absent from the TFOE panel, 0 targets) -- an absence of assay, so it neither supports nor refutes. Conclusion: on the evidence available, Rv3196A is not a WhiB7-regulon member, and its rank-2 induction in the alkX mutant has no established explanation. Note that the primary Mtb whiB7-regulon papers (Morris 2005 PNAS; Burian 2012 and 2013 JBC) could not be checked directly: their supplementary tables are behind PMC's download gate, with no preprint. Verdict and confidence are unchanged (dark, low) on purpose: secretion and antigenicity are a status, not a molecular function, and this atlas does not requalify a gene on a status.

Binding-pocket screen (P2Rank, geometric prediction) detector blind at this length

Read with care. This protein (66 aa) is below the size where this detector has meaningful power: on proven enzymes, only 3.8% (1/26) under 200 aa reach the P2Rank confidence threshold, versus 60.5% (75/124) above it (P16.3b calibration, negative control EsxA/EsxB-scale panel). A negative or weak pocket result here should NOT be read as evidence against a ligand-binding role -- the test essentially has no power at this length, not that the protein lacks a site. The same holds in the other direction, and that half was missing here: a STRONG score at this length is not a result either. The calibration below 200 aa measured only the detector's POWER -- how often a genuine site is recovered -- and never its false-positive rate, so nothing here says how often a score like this one appears on a protein that has no site at all. Read the number as untested in both directions. P16.3e, calibration phase72b_pocket_calibration.py, 2026-08-03.

Two orthogonal outgroup signals. Genus-wide presence/absence (tblastn vs assembled non-MTBC genomes) places the gene on the ancient-core ↔ MTBC-specific axis: a gene absent even from the closest MTBAP relatives is a candidate MTBC-specific innovation (possible host-adaptation factor, to confirm by synteny). The phylostratum extends that axis outside the genus (tblastn vs 13 reference genomes spanning Mycobacteriaceae → Corynebacteriales → Actinomycetia → outside the phylum): it is the deepest clade in which a homolog is still detected, i.e. a proxy for gene age. Read it with the null model in mind: a shallow (young) stratum can also reflect homology-detection failure for short or fast-evolving ORFs, so it is a descriptive axis, not a proof of novelty.

Detection by mass spectrometry is direct, experimental evidence that the protein product exists — orthogonal to sequence conservation and to Tn-seq essentiality, and especially decisive for a "hypothetical" locus. Read the dataset count as abundance, not as replication. Measured across this atlas (3,491 detected proteins), the number of datasets a protein appears in tracks its abundance almost monotonically: median 1.2 ppm at 1 dataset of 16, 27 ppm at 10, 392 ppm at 15, 1,325 ppm at 16 — three orders of magnitude. A high count therefore says the protein is abundant more than it says several independent experiments confirmed it. What the count does establish, at any value, is that the product exists.

Regulatory edges from the ISB signed transcriptional regulatory network (TF ChIP-seq binding, Minch 2015 + TF-overexpression response, Rustad 2014). An edge is regulatory evidence (binding and/or expression change), not necessarily direct. For a "hypothetical", membership in a known regulon (e.g. DosR dormancy, PhoP virulence) is a physiological-context lead, not a function.

Condition: Condition-blind by construction. The case for export therefore rests on the MHC class I result, which comes from a different group and a different method, not on the culture-filtrate detections.
