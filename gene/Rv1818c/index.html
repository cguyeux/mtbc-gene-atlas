Deep-dive (literature-grounded synthesis, hypothesis)

PE_PGRS33 is the best-studied PE_PGRS protein: surface-exposed, it engages macrophages via TLR2 (through its PGRS domain, residues ~140-260) to mediate mycobacterial entry, and has documented immunomodulatory, cytotoxic and antigenic effects in vitro -- but a knockout study gives a genuinely MIXED picture of its net contribution to virulence in vivo (no acute attenuation, and even enhanced tissue damage in the chronic phase), and this atlas's own MTBC-wide conservation data flag a candidate loss-of-function (frameshift) signal segregating in the global strain collection, consistent with an independent clinical-isolate finding.

Evidence
  • A PE_PGRS33-deficient M. tuberculosis H37Rv strain (MtbΔ33) enters macrophages ~10x less efficiently than wild type or the complemented strain, via a TLR2- and complement-receptor-3-dependent route; entry into non-phagocytic pneumocytes and intracellular growth rate are both unaffected; the PGRS domain (aa 140-260) is the functional determinant of entry (Palucci et al. 2016).
  • **Contrary to a simple 'virulence factor' framing**: in a murine infection model, the same MtbΔ33 knockout was NOT attenuated in acute lung infection, and showed ENHANCED tissue damage and virulence during the CHRONIC phase compared to wild type; natural clinical alleles carrying large in-frame or frameshift mutations failed to restore the macrophage-entry phenotype in human monocyte-derived macrophages, but this did not affect intracellular replication or immunomodulatory properties (Camassa et al. 2017). The same study found purifying selection (dN/dS=0.64) across 135 clinical isolates.
  • PE_PGRS33 localises to host-cell mitochondria upon expression and induces macrophage cell death (title-level citation, mechanism not re-derived here: Microbiology 2011, doi 10.1099/mic.0.041996-0).
  • Both the PGRS and PE domains are targets of humoral (antibody) responses in mice and humans (title-level citation: Frontiers in Immunology 2014, doi 10.3389/fimmu.2014.00236).
  • Natural clinical allelic variants differentially modulate TNF-α/IL-12/IL-10 induction upon macrophage infection, supporting a role in fine-tuning host response rather than a binary present/absent function (Camassa et al. 2017 and a 2022 J Infect Public Health follow-up on independent clinical isolates).
  • This atlas's own conservation layer independently flags a candidate pseudogene signal for PE_PGRS33 across the MTBC-wide strain collection (145,209 genomes): 2 frameshift variants among 14 SNP sites, alongside purifying selection overall -- directly consistent with the clinical frameshift alleles reported by Camassa et al. 2017 in an independent, much smaller collection, now corroborated at MTBC-wide scale.

Caveats: Do not state that PE_PGRS33 'is a virulence factor' as settled fact: the one in vivo knockout study found here shows a genuinely mixed, even partly OPPOSITE (enhanced chronic disease) phenotype, a textbook illustration of this project's family-wide anti-oversell guard-fou. The macrophage-ENTRY role (TLR2/CR3-dependent, PGRS-domain-mediated) is solid, knockout-and-complementation-tested evidence; the downstream virulence consequence is not. This atlas's own essentiality call is non-essential (Tn-seq, CRISPRi VI=0.14): consistent with the literature's growth-rate-neutral phenotype above.

Condition: Condition-blind by construction.
