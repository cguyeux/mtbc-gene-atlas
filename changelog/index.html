Changelog

This is a continuously updated resource: every deploy is a numbered version, and this page records what each one added, corrected, or removed. The live site is currently running v106 (2026-09-12). Per-gene diffs against the Mycobrowser baseline live in a separate per-gene changelog (CSV); this page tracks the site itself.

v106 — 2026-09-12

  • This site told machine consumers that 2946 proteins were cytoplasmic, on the strength of a predictor that never said so. The localisation layer reports the output of DeepTMHMM, which predicts membrane topology and Sec/Tat export signals. Its GLOB class means exactly one thing: no transmembrane helix and no signal peptide was found. This site translated that as "predicted cytoplasmic / globular" — a compartment the predictor does not measure, and an inference added here rather than a result. The evidence that it was wrong was already on this site: the 12 Esx paralogues of the atlas, including EsxA/ESAT-6 and EsxB/CFP-10, the best-established secreted proteins of M. tuberculosis, all carried that same label, because substrates of the type VII (ESX) secretion system leave no recognisable signal peptide. The label now reads "no signal peptide, no TM helix (DeepTMHMM class GLOB)". Scope, stated precisely: the phrase reached the REST API and the downloadable JSON only. The gene pages never displayed it, because the localisation section did not open on a bare GLOB call — so no page was showing a wrong compartment, and 2946 API records were.
  • Those 2946 pages were silent about localisation, and now say what was looked for and not found. A verified absence is information: each of these pages now states that no signal peptide, no transmembrane helix and no lipobox was found, and immediately adds the reservation that makes the statement usable — in this organism, "secreted" and "no signal peptide" are true together, as the Esx family shows. No cytoplasmic conclusion is drawn anywhere on the page.
  • Two proteins now carry a MEASURED localisation, with its method and its limits, next to the prediction rather than instead of it. Rv2569c is reported predominantly in the cell-wall fraction of H37Rv and at the cell surface by immunoelectron microscopy (Zang et al. 2024, PLoS Pathog 20(5):e1012214), and the page says that this does not contradict the prediction, which speaks of signals and topology and not of compartments. It also says what the measurement does not settle: the fractionation blot shows only Ag85B, itself a wall-associated and secreted marker, and no cytoplasmic marker (RpoB, GroEL2) — the control that decides whether a wall fraction is clean — so the wall/cytoplasm partition is weaker than the surface localisation, which rests on the microscopy. Rv1761c is the opposite case: solution NMR resolves one transmembrane helix plus a ~10 kDa extramembranous domain (Page et al. 2009, Protein Sci 18:134-146) where the predictor finds no helix at all, so the page says the experiment contradicts the prediction and that the structure is what should be read — while carrying the authors' own caveat that a detergent micelle is not a membrane. The prediction itself is kept exactly as produced in both cases: a layer whose provenance says DeepTMHMM must keep saying what DeepTMHMM said.
  • A JSON endpoint published in the manuscript had been answering 404 for six days, and answers again. /api/genes, cited in the paper as the companion endpoint for programmatic access, is still declared in the application and still works locally; the move to static hosting on 2026-09-06 simply stopped freezing it, and nothing noticed. It is frozen again, returns the complete collection, and says so — the q search parameter cannot be applied by a static host, and that is stated in the response rather than silently ignored. The published-URL check that was missing has been added to the deployment witness: from now on, every address cited in a paper, a README or a tool is verified on each release, not just the content of the pages.

v105 — 2026-09-12

  • Two screens disagreed on 119 more genes than this site admitted. The record already declared, on 125 pages, that the Tn-seq screen (DeJesus 2017) and the CRISPRi screen (Bosch 2021) do not agree — they do not measure the same thing, and a gene can absorb a transposon insertion yet still be required at full dosage. The detector that found those disagreements, however, only knew three of the Tn-seq calls. GD (growth-defect, 135 genes) and GA (growth-advantage, 300) appeared nowhere in it, although the data itself states that a GD gene is not essential: insertions are tolerated, fitness is merely reduced. So 119 pages presented the two screens side by side as if they concurred. 115 of them carry Bosch 2021's own certain flag, meaning the disagreement there is a real methodological tension rather than a weak fit — the very case the site previously described as limited to a dozen genes. Declarations rise from 125 to 244.
  • A third kind of disagreement, which is about scope rather than direction. For 27 genes, Tn-seq returns ESD: only a sub-region of the reading frame is required, and the gene as a whole is explicitly not called essential, while CRISPRi calls the gene essential. Both screens point to an important gene and differ on what is required. These pages now say so in those terms, and no longer under a heading claiming a plain contradiction.
  • "Essential" no longer means three different things in the same sentence. A derived field on these records groups ES, ESD and GD into one boolean, kept for backward compatibility — 164 genes are flagged that way without being strictly essential. The downloadable per-gene changelog named all of them "essential"; it now distinguishes essential, essential-domain and growth-defect. Two pages asserted essentiality without saying which screen supported it, and now name both the screen and the reach of the Tn-seq call: Rv2516c and Rv2195 (qcrA).
  • One measurement no longer outvotes nine — 37 phenotype summaries corrected. The in vivo phenotype summary declared a gene "required for fitness in vivo" as soon as a single measurement said so, however many said the opposite. Rv0502 carried that label on one depleted measurement against nine enriched ones; Rv2569c on one against seven. Unlike drug-exposure and stress phenotypes, which keep each condition with its own statement, these are replicate studies of the same condition and cannot be separated that way — so the summary now reports the split ("required in 1 of 10 measurements, disruption advantageous in 9") instead of resolving it by an implicit one-vote majority. Thanks are due to the sister project on Rv2569c, which located the cause precisely.
  • The downloadable changelog had been stale for weeks, and nothing would have caught it. It is a derived file, regenerated by hand, and it had not been regenerated since the conservation layer gained a statistical-power guard. That guard downgrades a "purifying selection" call to "quasi-invariant, underpowered" when two to seven variable sites cannot support the inference — so the file asserted purifying selection for 1014 genes where the layer itself no longer asserts it. Publication now refreshes its derived artefacts, the site database included, and refuses to proceed if that refresh fails. A downloaded file reaches its reader once and is never re-downloaded; it is the most durable kind of stale artefact.

v104 — 2026-09-11

  • Zero unresolved internal contradictions, for the first time. The intra-record coherence sweep counted 96 of them on 2026-09-09 and counts none today. The two campaigns that closed the gap are below; what they have in common is that measuring first changed what had to be fixed.
  • "Secreted" and "no signal peptide" are true together in this organism, and the site said otherwise. Fifteen records were flagged for sending a protein to the envelope while DeepTMHMM returned a globular prediction. Calibration on 21 published controls settled it: the layer finds the lipobox of the four acylated lipoproteins (LprG, LpqH, Mpt83, LprA), the Sec signal of the culture-filtrate antigens and the helices of MmpL3, Tap and P55 — and it calls the six canonical ESX substrates globular too, ESAT-6 and CFP-10 included. A globular prediction can contradict an asserted topology; it can never contradict secretion, because type VII secretion leaves no signature. Seven pages now say so instead of leaving the reader with two incompatible statements.
  • An experimental structure beats a prediction — Rv1761c. Solution NMR in DPC micelles resolves this 127-residue protein as one transmembrane helix plus a ~10 kDa extramembranous domain (Page et al. 2009). The DeepTMHMM call on this page is simply wrong, and the page now says which to read.
  • Two inherited "lipoprotein" names that nothing supports — Rv1899c (LppD) and Rv1946c (LppG). Of the 92 loci here whose inherited name says lipoprotein, 86 carry a predicted export signal and 4 more are membrane-embedded; these two are the only ones with neither. LppD turns out to be, in substance, a macrodomain (Pfam Macro) that the one primary study of the gene treats as such — and the whole lipoprotein question hangs on a start codon: RefSeq gives 343 residues, UniProt 359, and the 16 extra residues carry exactly the charged region a signal peptide needs.
  • Rv2862c loses a membrane it never had. "LiaF-like integral membrane protein" was a family-level inference twice over: no helix by either predictor, and Pfam says of the domain itself that every protein carrying it is uncharacterised.
  • Two EC numbers withdrawn. An EC number names a reaction. Rv1088a (60 residues) had inherited the cellulase reaction of a full-length glycoside hydrolase, and Rv2224d (73 residues) the reaction of a long-chain acyltransferase, both through a distant orthologue. The numbers are now shown as inherited and explicitly not adopted.
  • A caveat that only worked in one direction now works in both. For proteins under 200 residues, the pocket detector has no measured power, and the page already warned that a weak score proves nothing. It said nothing about a strong one — yet the calibration never measured the false-positive rate at that length either. All 71 short-protein pages now state both halves.
  • Two internal checks were reading less than the page says. The contradiction detector stripped quoted text so that a correction would not flag itself; its pattern also caught the English possessive apostrophe, and two distant possessives swallowed everything between them — 37 pages were being read at half length. And an "essential gene" check was counting calls that the same layer describes as not essential. Neither bug was visible from the outside; both were changing what the site said about itself.

v103 — 2026-09-10

  • A fourth channel: the name under which the literature actually cites a protein. The sweep behind every literature count searched three families of names — the H37Rv locus tag, the gene name the record carried, and the orthologue tags. It never searched the usage name: papers write MftA, DyP, HigA3, MtbVKOR, not the full UniProt protein name. Where a record had no gene name, the page ended up saying "no publication mentions this gene" about a protein the literature names.
  • The source of those names was already on disk, and was being discarded at parse time. The UniProt harvest never requested the gene_primary and gene_synonym fields, and cut the protein name at its first parenthesis — exactly where UniProt keeps synonyms: "Triple specificity protein phosphatase PtpB (MPtpB) (…)". 503 genes carry a UniProt gene name that had never been searched; restricted to pages whose label is falsifiable, this release covers 80 of them.
  • Five filters, because a short token lies in five different ways. A term shared with another locus is dropped before it is searched (MCM belongs to the neighbouring Rv1492); so is the bare form of a numbered name, which cannot tell paralogues apart (rpsR covers rpsR1 and rpsR2, cysA three loci); case is significant, so TACT (a DNA motif) and tACT (telehealth) are not tacT, and RIP3 (a human kinase) is not rip3; an activity abbreviation is reported but never counted; and every remaining record was re-read against the text PubMed indexes.
  • And then they were read one by one, because no rule catches everything. pgl returned 87 apparent hits that are all the phenolic glycolipid PGL-I of leprosy serology; the tadA records describe the Flp pilus operon, where Rv3752c is a tRNA adenosine deaminase. Eight pages therefore keep their label and now say why the count does not count.
  • 21 pages now say they are cited under their usage name, with the records listed and the name given: mftA (7 records), coaBC (8), fasR (6), cas6 (5), dyp (4), pabB, mce1E, fpg2, stf3, kefB, coaX, mbcA, MtbVKOR, sseC2, tacT, ybeY, rip3, fabG5, higA3, mddA, ipdC. The clearest case is Rv3238c, a "conserved hypothetical protein" whose function has been published since 2015: MddA, the methanethiol S-methyltransferase that makes dimethylsulphide, named in M. tuberculosis.
  • 36 pages have their silence confirmed on all four channels — locus tag, full protein name, orthologue tags and usage name. A verified absence is a result, and the page says so.

v102 — 2026-09-10

  • A publication count is not a count of publications about the gene. Yesterday's release put a warning on fifty-four pages whose header said no publication mentions the gene while their own sweep had counted some. Before letting that count rewrite the header, we checked what it counts. Rv2909c (rpsP) had 155, and the first was a study of mortality in a Brazilian HIV cohort: all 155 came from the single alias rpsP, matched against the DOI prefix of Rev Panam Salud Publica (10.26633/RPSP.…). PubMed's [All Fields] searches the DOI, the journal name, the affiliations and the author names as well as the text. Four more mechanisms, each verified in the raw records: the author Cobo for cobO (31 records), the author Moda for modA (8) — neither even an exact match —, "Str. Prov. per Casamassima" in an affiliation for proV (10), and the substance field "RpsI protein, E. coli" for rpsI (9).
  • All 264 records were re-read against the text a reader can read — title, abstract and author keywords — with the gene identifier required to appear as a whole token. A second filter proved necessary: of the 27 genes the text confirmed, 8 were homonyms in another organism (sseB and sthA of a Salmonella vaccine, ZWF1 of Candida, xylB of E. coli) or not gene names at all (RPSS, a surveillance system; RPLO, an entry in a list of abbreviations). The mycobacterial context the sweep required could be satisfied by the authors' affiliation alone; it must now be in the text itself.
  • Twenty pages did carry a false statement, and now say so. They are labelled as named in the mycobacterial literature under their gene name or locus tag, with the papers listed and the attribution left to the reader: naming is not attribution. Twenty-nine of yesterday's warnings have been withdrawn — they announced a disagreement between two counts, one of which measured nothing.
  • Where the count proves nothing, the page says what it is. Instead of "All 155 publications found for this gene", the list now opens with "155 records returned by the alias sweep — none names this gene", each row carries its own verdict (names it, same name, other organism, does not name it), and a note explains where the match actually fell.
  • Nothing was deleted to make a page tidier. Every rewritten summary is kept verbatim on the page it belonged to, with the date and where it was recovered from, and the explanation of what the label used to say and why it changed — written last week and never displayed — is now visible under the summary.

v101 — 2026-09-10

  • Fifty-four pages said no publication mentions the gene, while their own literature sweep had counted some — one of them 155. Each page carries two counts of the same thing, produced by different methods, and shows both rather than silently overwriting one with the other. A warning is meant to appear whenever they disagree. It could not appear on these fifty-four, because the test behind it required both counts to be non-zero, and the count in the page header was zero. The very case where the header is most clearly wrong was the one case the check could not see. Rv2909c (rpsP, 30S ribosomal protein S16) headed its page with zero publications while its own sweep, run on 7 September, had found 155.
  • The warning now appears on those pages, and it is legible. Once the check was fixed, the sentence it produces turned out to read "This count disagrees with the None reported above" — the same treat-zero-as-absent mistake, two lines further down in the same script, making the warning unreadable exactly where it had been invisible. Both are fixed; the sentence now names the figure.
  • Nothing was rewritten to make the disagreement go away. No publication count changed, on any page. Rewriting a figure produced by one method with a figure produced by another would hide the disagreement instead of showing it, and showing it is the point.
  • The check remains deliberately one-sided, and this is not an oversight. A page whose sweep found nothing while its inherited count is positive is still not flagged: that sweep reads titles and abstracts only, so finding nothing there proves nothing. Of the nine hundred and twenty-three differences the old test passed over, all but fifty-four were passed over for good reasons — small gaps, or that one-sided case.
  • Two pages lost an explanation they no longer needed. Rv0040c and Rv0798c had been given a note explaining why a label contradicted their structure; that label was corrected hours later, and the note was still quoting wording the page no longer showed. An explanation of a disagreement that has since been resolved is its own kind of error.

v100 — 2026-09-10

  • The explanatory notes added in v99 were written in French, on a site that is otherwise entirely in English. Fifty-one notes across the atlas — the forty-six published minutes earlier and five older ones — appeared inside the "Conflicting signals on this page" box as a paragraph most readers could not read, at exactly the point where the page was trying to explain a disagreement to them. All fifty-one are now in English. The five older ones were translated literally: every figure, reference and conclusion is carried across unchanged.
  • Six pages said "no publication mentions this gene" while listing publications they had themselves attributed to that gene. The label is derived from the locus-tag sweep alone, and never reads the protein-name sweep sitting a few sections below it in the same layer. Those six now read "cited in the literature under its protein name, not under its locus tag", and say which papers, under which name, and on what basis the attribution was made. Only the label changed: no count, no paper and no verdict was touched.
  • Four of those six were invisible to the check that found the problem. The contradiction surfaced through a rule that compares an experimental structure against an "unstudied" label, so it could only fire on genes that have a structure. Rv1321 (NucS), Rv0935, Rv0357c and Rv3542c have none, and carried the same defect unnoticed. Looking for the defect directly, rather than through the symptom that revealed it, is what found them.

v99 — 2026-09-10

  • Forty-six pages said "no publication mentions this gene" while showing an experimental structure of that same protein. Each now explains, in its own words, why both statements appear together. A solved structure means somebody purified this protein and published the work, so the two claims looked irreconcilable. Reading them one by one produced four different answers, not one.
  • Fourteen of them are simply named something else in the literature. The label is computed from the H37Rv locus tag and the ortholog tags; a companion sweep adds the full UniProt protein name. Neither sees the short name papers actually use. Rv1433 is cited as LdtMt3, never as "L,D-transpeptidase 3"; Rv0798c as Cfp29 or encapsulin, in seventeen abstracts; Rv0040c as MTC28, in eight. These pages now say so, with the name and the count, and tell the reader to read the label as "the tag is silent", never as "nobody has studied this protein".
  • Fourteen others carry a structure they are not the subject of. They are ribosomal proteins resolved inside whole-ribosome cryo-EM maps. A subunit appearing in an assembly is not a study of that subunit, so "a structure exists" and "no publication names this gene" are both true. The page now says which of the two is which.
  • Six are genuinely unstudied, and that is now a measured statement rather than a default. For Rv1445c (DevB), Rv1493 (MutB), Rv2277c (GlpQ), Rv2717c, Rv2740 (EphG) and Rv2908c (KhpA), the silence holds under all three channels — locus tag, full protein name, and short usage name. A verified absence is information: it flags an annotation with no primary study behind it. The last twelve have no usage name distinct from their tag, so the third channel cannot apply, and the page says that too.
  • One measurement was wrong before it was right, and the correction is worth stating. Searching grouped name variants counted a neighbouring gene: querying LdtMt3 OR LdtMt2 returned 38 papers for Rv1433, but LdtMt2 is a different locus, and the honest count is 3. Two other genes flipped the other way, from an apparent 13 and 24 papers down to none, once family and activity names were excluded. Only names unique to a gene are counted here; family names are reported and never counted, because they cannot tell one paralogue from another.

v98 — 2026-09-10

  • Eight genes served as functionally unknown, while this atlas's own literature layer called them "functional", now say which of the two is right — and it is the verdict. Read one by one, the evidence behind that label turned out to be a genome-wide screen, a genetic-requirement screen, a regulon membership, a co-immunoprecipitation partner, a comparative transcription study, or a position in an operon. None of these is a molecular function: a phenotype is not a function, and being co-transcribed with a gene is not being that gene. Flipping the eight verdicts would have requalified, in one pass, eight genes nobody knows the job of. Each page now states what the label rests on, with its PubMed identifier, and why the verdict stands.
  • One of those eight was hiding something real. Rv0426c read "function unknown" although a study dedicated to this gene reports that expressing it in a recombinant mycobacterium promotes intracellular survival by modulating host inflammatory cytokines (PubMed 31614213). That is a phenotype obtained in a surrogate host, not a molecular function of the M. tuberculosis protein — the verdict is unchanged, but the sentence now cites the study and says exactly what it does and does not establish.
  • "Absent from all 53 non-MTBC mycobacterial genomes" does not mean "absent from the genus", and six pages now say so. The panel behind that statement is made of slow growers and close relatives: it contains neither M. smegmatis nor M. abscessus. Six records claimed MTBC specificity while their own orthology layer named an ortholog in exactly those unlisted species — most strikingly Rv2737c (recA), 93 % identical to its M. smegmatis counterpart. The two layers never disagreed; they interrogated different genomes, and the page now states which.
  • Three pages carried a badge their own text refuted. Rv2274c, Rv2341 and Rv3852 were labelled MTBC-specific while the same section read "NOT a robust MTBC-specific innovation — present but divergent". The downgrade had been computed and written months ago, but only into a neighbouring field; it now reaches the label, with the previous value kept for traceability.
  • The site is reachable over HTTPS again. Since the move to static hosting, https://mtbc.gclab.fr had been failing on a certificate mismatch while plain HTTP worked — which broke the link printed in the manuscript and the API endpoint used by client tools. The certificate is issued, HTTPS is enforced, and HTTP now redirects to it.

v97 — 2026-09-09

  • The atlas now says when its own evidence layers disagree, instead of leaving the reader to notice. A gene page carries some thirty layers, written by different passes months apart, and two of them can point in opposite directions on the same screen. A systematic pass over all 3,974 pages (25 rules, each calibrated on a synthetic positive and negative control) found 339 such places. Where two measurements genuinely disagree, the page now declares it — next to the number the reader is about to trust, not two sections further down — and says what to make of the disagreement.
  • Essentiality: the two genome-wide screens disagree on 125 genes, and the pattern is not what a single sentence would have said. Tn-seq (DeJesus 2017) measures tolerance to transposon insertion; CRISPRi (Bosch 2021) measures tolerance to partial knockdown. All 101 genes called non-essential by Tn-seq and essential by CRISPRi carry the CRISPRi authors' own "not certain" flag on that estimate — every single one, against 85 % across the atlas — so that direction of disagreement rests on weak fits rather than on biology, and each page now says so. The 12 genes where Tn-seq calls essential and a confident CRISPRi call says otherwise are the genuine methodological tension, and they are labelled as such.
  • Disorder: 55 proteins are called highly disordered from their sequence while their AlphaFold model is confident. These are dominated by low-complexity sequence: 38 of the 55 are PE/PPE proteins (69 %, against 4.0 % atlas-wide, hypergeometric p = 1.8e-42), with a median alanine+glycine+proline content of 0.55 against 0.28 elsewhere. Neither predictor is wrong; the page now declines to present either number as a settled statement about the protein's shape.
  • Five gene pages placed the same CDS on opposite strands in their header and in their neighbourhood view. Arbitrated against both reference annotations and corrected (Rv0814c, Rv0815c, Rv1038c, Rv1047, Rv1199c); the cause was a re-anchoring step that moved coordinates without moving the strand, now fixed at the source. On three further pages (Rv0943c, Rv1313c, Rv2812) the same discrepancy turned out to be a symptom of a known imperfect ancestral anchor, and is declared rather than papered over.
  • Two co-transcription claims are downgraded from statement to hypothesis. Rv2481c and Rv2767c asserted co-transcription with a named neighbour as a fact, while the one published operon map covering the pair places that neighbour in another transcript. The claim is not refuted — that map agrees with only 5 of our 12 operon-based calls, no better than chance — but it is unsupported, and the sentence now says which is which.

v96 — 2026-09-08

  • A hypothesis published yesterday is withdrawn today, by this atlas's own data. The v95 page for Rv3196A raised an open question: the RNA-seq table that shows this gene strongly induced is dominated by the WhiB7 regulon, so is Rv3196A a member of it? The answer is no, and the test that settles it was already in the atlas. The co-expression layer (647 RNA-seq samples, iModulonDB) contains a WhiB7 iModulon of 8 genes that recovers the canonical set — whiB7 itself, eis, erm(37), Rv1258c, Rv0263c. Rv3196A is not in it; its only iModulon is Unc_4, which contains none of those. The positive control matters: the WhiB7 signature is detectable in this compendium, so the test could have said yes.
  • A chromosomal statement on the same page was true but incomplete, and is corrected. v95 noted that Rv3196A sits about 1.5 kb from whiB7 on the same strand. It does — but Rv3197 lies between them on the opposite strand (gaps of 127 and 33 bp), so transcriptional read-through from the whiB7 locus is not a simple explanation. The page now says so.
  • What remains, stated as what it is. Rv3196A is the second most induced gene of 41 in an alkX deletion mutant (log2 fold change 3.76, adjusted P = 1.8e-30), and that induction now has no established explanation. The page records it as an unexplained fact rather than dressing it in a mechanism.
  • The culture-filtrate evidence added in v95 is downgraded, because it was measured. v95 said Rv3196A is "probably exported", resting partly on its M. marinum orthologue being found in a secreted protein fraction, with a qualitative caveat about lysis carry-over. That caveat has now been quantified across the whole dataset: in that fraction, predicted-cytoplasmic globular proteins are detected at 42.1 % and predicted signal-peptide proteins at 42.0 % — no enrichment whatsoever (Fisher P = 0.07) — while detection tracks abundance instead (80.6 % of globular proteins in the most abundant quartile). RpoB and GroEL2, both strictly cytoplasmic, are in the table. Rv3196A sits in that top abundance quartile, so its presence in the filtrate is what its abundance alone predicts. The page now reads "plausibly but not demonstrably exported", and the case for export rests on the MHC class I result, which comes from a different group and a different method.
  • What the same dataset does establish for this gene is a negative, and it stands: whatever puts the M. marinum orthologue in that fraction, it is not ESX-1.
  • Everything else on the page is unchanged: the gene remains dark, with no molecular function assigned, and the MHC class I antigenicity evidence added in v95 stands.
  • Two sentences the proteomics section had been serving on every page are now measured, and one of them was misleading. It said that reproducible detection across several independent datasets makes the existence claim robust. Across the 3,491 detected proteins, the number of datasets a protein appears in tracks abundance almost monotonically — median 1.2 ppm at 1 dataset of 16, 27 ppm at 10, 1,325 ppm at 16, three orders of magnitude — so a high count says "abundant" at least as much as it says "independently confirmed". The page now says so; what the count does establish, at any value, is that the protein product exists.
  • The clade attribution shipped this morning was naming lineages it had no right to name. When a page said a disrupting site is "fixed in L4", the underlying code had recorded the root of the fine clade that fixes it — so a site fixed in a single sub-clade of 5 strains out of 54,539 was displayed as an L4-wide loss. Across the 789 fixed sites in this atlas, the sub-clades that actually fix a site cover a median of 9.2 % of the lineage named, and under 10 % in half the cases. Pages now read, for example, "fixed in 136 sub-clades of Bovis, L4, L5, L6, L_1, La4, totalling 13,507 strains — 18.3 % of the 73,634 strains in the lineages named", and name the largest sub-clades with their carrier counts.
  • The disruption percentage now comes with its ceiling. The strain collection is deeply imbalanced: a gene lost in every L6 strain still caps at 0.96 % of the collection, in every M. caprae strain at 0.20 %, in every M. microti strain at 0.02 %, and 689 of the 705 clades have a ceiling below 1 %. "Disrupted in 0.15 % of strains" is therefore uninterpretable on its own — noise, or a complete loss defining an entire animal ecotype. The section says this, on the pages that have a disruption site.
  • And the reason a protein goes undetected was attributed to the wrong cause. The page listed small, highly hydrophobic or repetitive proteins. Measured on the 482 undetected ones, size dominates: median 145 aa versus 295 aa, 25.3 % shorter than 100 aa versus 6.0 %. Predicted localisation matters much less (89.3 % of globular proteins detected, 84.9 % of transmembrane ones), and signal-peptide proteins are the least detected class at 77.8 % — which a hydrophobicity story does not predict. The page now names size first and gives the four rates.

That detection is now reported as cited by Sher et al. and not verified here.
  • The M. marinum orthologue, by contrast, is now verified in the primary table. Cronin et al. (PNAS 2022, ESX-1 proteo-genetics, open access) was located and its Dataset S2 read: MMAR_1367 (UniProt B2HFI1) is quantified in the secreted protein fraction of all 13 mutant-versus-wild-type comparisons — and its secretion is not ESX-1-dependent (log2 fold change -0.11 to +0.80, P >= 0.46, the authors' own threshold never met, including in the deltaeccCb1, deltaesxA and deltaesxB strains). A positive result and a negative one, neither of which appears in any abstract. The page also states the standard caveat: a secreted fraction can carry cytoplasmic protein released by lysis, so this is consistent with export rather than proof of it.
  • One of the papers the previous release filed as a passing table mention was not one. In Microbiology Spectrum 2022, Rv3196A is the second most upregulated gene of 41 in an alkX deletion mutant (log2 fold change 3.76, adjusted P = 1.8e-30) — the strongest transcriptional fact known for this locus. The general point of v94 stands in aggregate: being named is not being studied. It does not survive gene by gene, and the pages that depend on it say so now.
  • What the page still refuses to say. Antigenicity and export are a status, not a molecular function. The verdict stays dark with low confidence, and no function is assigned. An open hypothesis is recorded as such, with the two checks that failed to support it: Rv3196A sits about 1.5 kb from whiB7 on the same strand, and three canonical WhiB7-regulon genes occupy ranks 3, 5 and 7 of that same table.

v94 — 2026-09-08

  • "No publication mentions this gene" was wrong on 154 pages, and the fix distinguishes two things the site had been conflating. For genes with no hit in either abstract corpus, the page used to conclude that the gene was genuinely unstudied. A full-text search — the same Europe PMC channel the atlas already used on 1,089 other genes — finds at least one paper naming 154 of the 179 such genes (median 3 papers, up to 23). Those pages now list what was found and no longer make the claim.
  • Being NAMED is not being STUDIED, and the distinction is the point. The papers are overwhelmingly large-scale screens: drug-resistance surveys, comparative genomics, GWAS and pangenome studies, proteomics, whole-genome sequencing. The gene appears in a supplementary list, not in an investigation. So the atlas still holds that this residue is genuinely dark in the sense that no one has studied these genes — what it withdraws is the broader wording, which a reader could refute with a single Europe PMC query. 23 genes remain silent across all three channels; those are the ones for which an absence can now be stated without reservation.
  • One gene came out of this with real biology attached. Rv3196A — 66 amino acids, no known domain, abundant (733 ppm, 91.5th percentile, seen in 14 of 16 proteomics datasets) and conserved across the genus (31 of 53 non-tuberculous mycobacteria) — is shown by immunopeptidomics (Sher et al., eLife 2023) to have peptides presented on MHC class I during infection, and had been detected by mass spectrometry in culture filtrates (Bell et al. 2012), as had its M. marinum orthologue. The authors conclude it is likely secreted despite lacking any recognisable secretion signal — which this atlas's own localisation layer independently reports. Its molecular function is still unknown, so the verdict stays dark; what changed is that "never observed" no longer holds.

v93 — 2026-09-08

  • Which clade carries which disrupting site — the question the previous counter never asked. The conservation layer reported how many premature-stop or frameshift sites a gene carries, and called the gene convergent as soon as there were four of them. That word is an evolutionary claim, but the calculation was a count: it never looked at who carries what, nor at whether the sites differ between clades. A companion project read "one site dominates in 92.6 % of strains" on Rv2566 and concluded, for a year, a loss of function specific to M. bovis. Attributing the sites shows three fixations in three DIFFERENT animal ecotypes — Bovis (13,001 strains), M. orygis (353) and M. caprae (280): convergence, the opposite of the story the page allowed.
  • The count was wrong in both directions, and that is now visible on 452 gene pages. Of the 105 genes the layer called convergent, 85 are confirmed, 10 have only a single site actually fixed in a clade, 6 are INTRA-clade convergence (several sites, one ecotype — a real but different phenomenon), and 4 have no fixed site at all. Meanwhile the attribution finds 214 inter-clade convergences in total, so roughly 130 were being missed. Each affected page now carries the clades involved, the true union of carriers (the older figure is a maximum over sites, not a union), and, per site, the clades in which it is fixed.
  • The distinction the new field enforces. Several clades sharing the SAME site is shared ancestry, not convergence; convergence requires DIFFERENT sites in DIFFERENT clades. Without that rule the field would manufacture convergences in bulk. A clade counts as carrying a site when at least half of its strains have it, and the finding is robust to that choice: 240 convergences at a 30 % threshold, 214 at 50 %, 182 at 80 %, 150 at 95 % — above the 105 previously declared at every setting. The original disrupt_mode, disrupt_sites and disrupt_strains fields are left untouched, side by side with the new reading.
  • Conservation figures now say when they were computed. The heavy pass over the strain collection is rarely re-run while the collection itself keeps changing, so each page now states the snapshot date (currently 2026-06-03, 145,209 strains) next to the strain count. This explains, for instance, why Rv2566 shows 14,039 strains for its commonest site while a fresh recount over today's collection gives 13,786.

  • Operon-based requalifications are re-judged against two published operon maps instead of one. An earlier audit reported that 8 of the 12 dark genes requalified through a co-transcription argument were contradicted by Roback et al. 2007. Each fiche now carries both verdicts together with their base rates over all adjacent gene pairs of the genome (Roback 26.3 %, REMap 45.6 %), without which an agreement cannot be read: REMap agrees more often than chance on this set (9/10, one-sided binomial p = 0.005), Roback does not (5/12, p = 0.19).
